# Cinematic Dark-Luxury Food Reveal

## 1. When to use this pattern

**Best fit:**
- Premium food or beverage products: burgers, steaks, sushi, chocolate, coffee, spirits,
  artisan bread, and similar.
- Brands where the product itself is the hero -- no spokesperson, no lifestyle B-roll needed.
- Quality-certification positioning: halal, kosher, organic, farm-to-table, hand-crafted.
- Platform-agnostic 16:9 hero content (YouTube pre-roll, paid social, brand website, OOH).
- Audiences who respond to aspiration and craft: foodies, premium QSR, upmarket DTC.

**Poor fit:**
- Brands needing a human face, influencer energy, or UGC authenticity.
- Playful or budget-conscious positioning.
- Vertical short-form (TikTok/Reels) where cinematic widescreen grammar is lost.

---

## 2. Hook and Opening

**Structural move: Macro-texture pattern interrupt**

Open on an extreme close-up of the product's most viscerally compelling detail -- sizzling,
melting, crisping, pouring. No context, no brand name, no full-product reveal. Pure sensory
provocation. The viewer cannot look away because they cannot yet identify what they are
watching.

**Example opening scene (generic):**
> Scene 1: Melting chocolate cascading over a single truffle -- extreme close-up, warm
> specular highlights catching the glaze, anamorphic lens flares, soft cocoa dust drifting
> through the frame.

**Swap rule:** Replace the cascading chocolate with your hero ingredient (sizzling patty,
espresso crema, steaming ramen broth, cascading cheese). Keep the extreme close-up, the
heat or motion, and the anamorphic flare. Never open on a full-product establishing shot --
that is a payoff, not a hook.

**Opening voice-over rhythm (spoken, not shown on screen):**
> "Some flavors are made. This one is earned. Slow-crafted, never rushed."

Structure: short poetic sensory claim followed by a craft credential. Always two sentences.
Never more than two on the opening beat.

---

## 3. Narrative Arc

**Beat 1 -- SENSORY PROVOCATION (6-8 seconds)**
Visual: Macro ingredient in motion (sizzle, melt, pour, bubble).
Voice-over: Short poetic claim plus craft credential.
Energy: Tension, mystery, pull. Viewer is hooked before they understand why.

**Beat 2 -- INGREDIENT INTEGRITY (7-9 seconds)**
Visual: Slow-motion assembly sequence -- components arriving in deliberate order.
Voice-over: Sourcing or quality claim plus flavor or experience promise.
Energy: Reverent, deliberate, building. The product is being constructed before us.

**Beat 3 -- HERO REVEAL (7-9 seconds)**
Visual: Full product turntable on a dark background, signature money-detail visible (cheese
pull, sauce drip, foam bloom, cross-section layers).
Voice-over: Brand name drop plus positioning line.
Energy: Payoff, pride, luxury. This is the first time the viewer sees the whole product.

**Beat 4 -- BRAND CARD, SILENT (5-7 seconds)**
Visual: Dark textured background (charcoal, slate, matte black). Brand name in gold
typography. Tagline in serif below. Smoke wisps or subtle atmosphere optional.
Voice-over: None. Music fades softly or sustains at low volume.
Energy: Gravitas, memorability. The brand name is the last image burned in.

Total target duration: 25-34 seconds.

## 4. Visual Style Spec

Aesthetic: Dark luxury cinematic. The reference anchors are premium documentary-style food
cinematography and late high-end product commercials -- rich blacks, warm specular highlights,
deliberate camera grammar.

Resolution: 8K hyper-realistic render target.
Aspect ratio: 16:9 widescreen, 1920x1080.

Color palette:
- Background and shadows: deep charcoal blacks (approximately #1A1A1A).
- Product highlights: warm amber and bronze tones.
- Accent: gold (#D4A017 or equivalent warm gold).
- Steam, foam, smoke: cream white with soft diffusion.

Lighting: Single-source dramatic side or back lighting. Rim light separates the product from
background. No flat frontal fill. Hard shadows are acceptable; flat shadows are not.

Lens grammar: Anamorphic throughout. Lens flares appear on specular product highlights (oil
sheen, chrome, glaze). Shallow depth of field with soft bokeh backgrounds. The lens should
feel expensive.

Camera movement: Slow push-ins, deliberate circular arcs, locked-off macro for Beat 1.
No handheld shake. No fast cuts in Beats 1 through 3. Rhythm is meditative, not kinetic.

Shot length: Long takes (4-8 seconds each). Fast cutting is not this pattern.

Slow motion: Required for Beat 2 assembly sequences. Optional on Beat 1 for sizzle or
pour effects.

Atmosphere: Volumetric steam, smoke wisps, floating micro-particulate (seasoning, flour
dust, crumbs) are strongly encouraged. They add depth and luxury texture.

On-screen text policy: Minimal. Captions only during voice-over beats (editorial-clean
preset, serif typeface, word-by-word reveal). No large graphic overlays except the optional
hook line.

Hook overlay (optional, Beat 1): If used, a two-line minimal text overlay at the top of
frame. Example (generic): "More Than A Meal. / A Ritual." Style: minimal,
no color fill, no drop shadow. Position: approximately 18 percent from top of frame
(position_y_ratio 0.18).

Brand card typography:
- Brand name: condensed sans-serif display face, gold (#D4A017), horizontally centered.
- Tagline: editorial serif, warm white, lower-center position below brand name.
- No auto-captions on the brand card beat (mute captions for this beat).

## 5. Voice and Persona

Archetype: Calm-authoritative narrator. Not a hype announcer. Not a conversational peer.
The register is closer to a nature-documentary narration or a luxury watchmaker
brand voice -- unhurried, each word chosen.

Tone: Deep, warm, deliberate. Pauses between sentences are intentional and should not be
edited out. The silence between lines is part of the pacing.

Voice profile: Deep male, middle-aged, confident. Warm resonance, not booming. Swap to a
warm female voice for brands with a different audience positioning -- keep the
calm-authoritative register regardless of gender.

Pacing: Measured. The narrator never rushes. The brand name on its first appearance should
receive a micro-pause before it and a slight added weight on delivery.

Sample phrasing templates (swap product/brand specifics, keep the rhythm):

Beat 1 template:
"Every [sensory noun] tells a story. Crafted with [craft virtue], perfected with [second
craft virtue]."

Beat 2 template:
"Premium [ingredient descriptor] ingredients, sourced with [integrity word]. [Flavor
adjective] flavors -- a [experience adjective] and unforgettable experience."

Beat 3 template:
"This is more than a [product category]. This is [Brand Name] -- where [brand axis 1]
meets [brand axis 2]."

Hard rule on language: Never use superlatives (the best, world-class, number one,
unmatched). Use specificity and sensory language instead. "Sourced with integrity" is
stronger than "the highest quality."

---

## 6. CTA Mechanic

Type: Soft brand-name close. No direct action prompt. No URL. No swipe-up. No order-now.

The CTA is the brand card itself (Beat 4). The brand name in gold on a dark background,
held for 5-7 seconds with near-silence, does the work. This is not a weakness -- luxury
brands close on identity, not on urgency.

If a spoken CTA is explicitly required by the brief, append a single soft declarative
sentence at the very end of Beat 3 voice-over, before the silent card:
"[Brand Name]. Now open." or "Find us at [location/platform]."

Never overlay a promotional call-to-action on the brand card itself. A URL or "Order Now"
badge on the brand card degrades the luxury register and should be refused.

---

## 7. Hard Rules and Do-Not-Regress

These rules are non-negotiable in all future applications of the pattern.

**Rule 1: Multi-layer product specs must be visually confirmed in every scene.**
If the brief specifies double patty, double shot, dual layer, two scoops, or any
multi-component product, every scene that shows the product must make those components
visually distinct and countable. A single-component shot in a multi-component ad is a
product accuracy failure, not an acceptable approximation.

**Rule 2: Side-profile camera angle is mandatory for multi-component stacks.**
When the product has stacked layers (patty stack, cake tiers, layered cocktail, sandwich
cross-section), the camera must hold a profile angle that makes all layers simultaneously
visible. Overhead or three-quarter angles that obscure the layering violate the product
accuracy rule. Camera arcs must be designed to never rotate past a point where the
layer count becomes ambiguous.

**Rule 3: Assembly sequence must follow correct build order.**
Beat 2 shows components arriving in the sequence they are built. First component down
first; final component lands last. This communicates craft and intentionality. An
assembly sequence that shows components appearing in random or reversed order undermines
the craftsmanship narrative.

**Rule 4: No auto-captions on the brand card beat.**
Mute caption generation for the final beat entirely. The brand name and tagline as
designed typography are the only text permitted. Auto-captions collide with the locked
typographic layout and degrade the luxury finish.

**Rule 5: Music volume must stay background-level.**
Mix ratio of 0.20 or lower. The cinematic orchestral score supports the voice-over and the
silence of the brand card -- it does not compete with either. If in doubt, go quieter.

**Rule 6: Hook overlay text stays at the top of frame, minimal style.**
If a hook text overlay is used (Beat 1), it belongs near the top of frame
(position_y_ratio approximately 0.18) so it stays clear of the product hero. No drop
shadows, no color fill boxes, no bold borders. The text should feel like an editorial
caption, not a YouTube thumbnail.
