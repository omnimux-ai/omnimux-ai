# Cinematic Warm-to-Dark Safety Arc

## 1. When to use this pattern

**Suits:**
- Personal safety, emergency response, health-alert, lone-worker, or family-tracking apps
- Products with a single covert or one-gesture mechanic (hold a button, tap twice, shake the phone)
- Dense urban markets where personal safety anxiety is culturally salient
- TikTok and Instagram Reels (9:16 vertical), 15–65 second runtime
- Audiences: women 18-40, parents of teenagers, adult children of elderly parents, night-shift or field workers

**Does not suit:**
- B2B SaaS, fashion, food, or any product where fear is not a legitimate motivator
- Ads where the product's value requires extended explanation (use a different explainer pattern instead)
- Horizontal formats or broadcast TV — the intimacy of handheld 9:16 is load-bearing here

---

## 2. Hook & Opening (0–5 s)

**Structural move: Covert-action POV reveal.**

The very first shot shows the product's *gesture* or *trigger* being performed — without naming the product or explaining anything. The viewer sees the action; context arrives in the second beat. This creates a micro-mystery that compels watching.

**Invented example (swap the product, keep the move):**
> *[Shot: a hand, concrete wall behind it, presses a side button twice in silence. No VO. No title card.]*

**Rule:** Whatever your product's covert or activation mechanic is, open on it. Show the hand, the gesture, the screen flash — not a logo. The logo and product name belong in beat 3 or later.

**Swap guidance:** Replace "press a side button twice" with your product's gesture. Keep the low-angle close-up, the ambient sound, and the silence before VO begins.

---

## 3. Narrative Arc

Beat-by-beat shape: **Silent-trigger → Dread escalation → Product resolution → Social proof whisper → CTA**

| Beat | Target duration | Visual tone | VO/audio tone |
|------|----------------|-------------|---------------|
| **1. Silent trigger** | 0–4 s | Warm ambient (golden, amber) or neutral day light. Close-up of the gesture. No VO. | Ambient sound only — footsteps, background hum. |
| **2. Dread escalation** | 4–14 s | Lighting cools to blue-grey or deep shadow. Wider shots showing isolation, threat context (empty carpark, dark stairwell, unfamiliar street). | VO enters low and measured (e.g. *"Most emergencies don't wait for you to be ready."*). Pacing slow — 1.5–2.0 words/second. |
| **3. Product resolution** | 14–28 s | UI insert or screen flash (app dashboard, GPS ping animation). Warm amber creeps back into frame as the product "saves" the situation. | VO names product and core mechanic. Word count must stay within 1.5–3.0 w/s band per scene duration. |
| **4. Scenario expansion** | 28–50 s *(optional; use for 45 s+ ads only)* | Cut to 2–3 quick vignettes (elderly parent, teen walking home, night-shift worker) — each 3–5 s, same warm↔cool lighting arc. | VO or on-screen text names each audience segment. Keep vignettes silent or near-silent; let the visual arc carry the emotion. |
| **5. Social proof whisper** | (last 6–10 s before CTA) | Warm-lit close-up — subject relaxed, half-smile. | One line, conversational, slightly conspiratorial (e.g. *"Everyone I care about is on it now."*). Anchor value as a concrete figure, not a percentage. |
| **6. CTA** | Final 4–6 s | Clean title card or minimal on-screen text. Mute captions on this scene. | Spoken CTA + referral mechanic if applicable. Soft sign-off, never shouted. |

## 4. Visual Style Spec

| Parameter | Spec |
|-----------|------|
| **Format** | 9:16 vertical, 1080×1920 |
| **Lens grammar** | 85mm–equivalent, shallow DoF (f/1.8–2.8 feel), anamorphic lens flare on highlights |
| **Camera movement** | Scenes 1–2: locked-off or very slow push-in. Scenes 3–4: subtle handheld drift (not shaky — intimate). Closing: locked-off. |
| **Shot length** | Opening: 3–5 s. Dread: 4–6 s per cut. Resolution: 4–7 s. Vignettes: 3–4 s. CTA: 4–6 s. Average ~4.5 s/shot. |
| **Lighting arc** | **Warm amber (3200 K)** in safety moments → **Cool blue-grey (5600–6500 K)** in threat moments → warm returns with resolution. This is the emotional signature — do not flatten it to neutral throughout. |
| **Film grain** | Light grain (ISO 400 feel) on all scenes. Removes TikTok "ad" feel; adds urgency authenticity. |
| **Color palette** | Threat scenes: desaturated navy + concrete grey + single accent (phone screen glow). Safety scenes: amber, warm white, soft skin tones. |
| **On-screen text** | Use a serif typeface for warm/emotional scenes. A clean sans-serif for price/feature callouts. Never mix within a single scene. |
| **Caption style** | Night/threat scenes: `glow` preset. Warm family scenes: `editorial-clean`. Subscription/pricing explainers: `clean-bold`. |
| **UI inserts** | Show real app screens (dashboard, GPS ping, login) as phone-in-hand inserts, not floating overlays. Duration: 2–3 s each. |

## 5. Voice & Persona

**Tone:** Calm-authoritative with a conspiratorial edge. Not alarming — the voice sounds like a trusted older sibling who has already sorted this out and is letting you in on the secret.

**Persona archetype:** *Peer-with-knowledge* — on-camera talent (if used) is late-20s to early-30s, urban, dressed practically (dark windbreaker, jeans), not aspirationally. They are *not* a spokesperson; they are someone who uses this thing.

**Persona spec (reusable template):**
- Late 20s–early 30s, adapt ethnicity/appearance to the local target market
- Dark, practical outerwear — no brand logos
- Setting: real urban environments (parking lot, stairwell, kitchen at night, bus stop)
- Expression: composed, not performative

**Speech pacing:** 1.5–2.5 words/second in dread beats; up to 3.0 w/s in resolution/feature beats. Always verify word count against scene duration before finalising VO script.

**Sample phrasing rhythms (invent your own; these are shape examples):**
- *"In five seconds — before you can even react — [product name] has already sent your location."*
- *"It doesn't ring. It doesn't buzz. It just… works."*
- State prices as a spoken number plus the currency word (e.g. *"five a month, per person"* with the currency word spelled out), never as a bare symbol-prefixed figure.

**Pronunciation hard rule:** Write all currency in VO scripts as a number followed by the currency word spelled out (e.g. "15 dollars", "15 euros") — never as a symbol- or letter-prefixed figure (e.g. "$15", "R15"). TTS engines mispronounce or skip symbol/letter-prefixed amounts.

## 6. CTA Mechanic

**Pattern: Referral-first soft close.**

Lead with the referral reward before the product price. This reframes the CTA from "spend money" to "earn something."

**Structure:**
1. State the referral benefit first: *"Refer a friend — they pay [discounted price], you get a free month."*
2. Then state core pricing: *"[Full price] a month, or [family price] for [N] people."*
3. Soft spoken sign-off (no hard sell): *"Link in bio. Stay safe."*
4. Final title card: Product name + tagline only. No cluttered list of features.

**CTA scene rules:**
- Mute/suppress captions on the final CTA scene — let the title card breathe
- On-screen text uses a clean sans-serif, white on dark background, left-aligned
- No animation on the final card — static hold for 3–5 s

---

## 7. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

Treat these as non-negotiable.

1. **Currency TTS rule:** All monetary values in VO scripts must be written as a number followed by the currency word spelled out (e.g. "15 dollars per person"), never as a symbol- or letter-prefixed figure (e.g. "$15", "R15"). TTS engines mispronounce the symbol/letter prefix.
2. **Family plan anchoring:** When the product has a family/group plan, anchor the per-person price, not the total. *"15 dollars per person"* not *"75 dollars a month"*.
3. **Referral is a hero mechanic, not a footnote:** The bring-a-friend/referral reward belongs in the CTA *opening line*, not as a throwaway at the end.
4. **Lighting arc must be directional:** Each scene brief must specify a lighting temperature/direction (warm → cool or cool → warm). Flat neutral lighting across all scenes breaks the emotional arc.
5. **VISUAL DESCRIPTION LOCK for brand assets:** When inspected product assets (UI screenshots, logos, hero images) are available, every director scene brief must include a `VISUAL DESCRIPTION LOCK` section that describes those assets by appearance so the director does not hallucinate or substitute generic imagery.
6. **Word count verification:** Before finalising any VO script, verify that the word count fits the scene duration at 1.5–3.0 words/second. Scenes that run long cause rushed delivery and mis-timed cuts.
7. **No caption on CTA scene:** The closing title-card scene must have `mute_captions: true` (or equivalent) to prevent caption text from crowding the card.
8. **Serif for emotional scenes only:** Do not use a serif typeface on pricing, feature-list, or CTA scenes. Those use a clean sans-serif.
9. **Persona is voice_only for narrator-led ads:** If no on-camera talent appears, set `voice_only: true` in persona setup to avoid generating an unused avatar.
10. **Music arc must match emotional arc:** Music brief should specify a warm-building-to-tense-to-resolving shape. Do not use a single flat-energy track across all ad variants — brief each track to the specific ad's emotional journey.
