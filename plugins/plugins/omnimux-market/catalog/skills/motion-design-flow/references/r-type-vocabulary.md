# R-Type Brand Reveal Vocabulary

**v1.8.0 doctrine file.** Extended brand reveal taxonomy for motion design endcards. R-types describe how the brand wordmark resolves into the final composition — material relationship between wordmark and the world established across the preceding panels.

Use R-type vocabulary when writing the brand panel (final panel of the reel). Pick ONE R-type per reel — mixing R-types breaks endcard discipline.

---

## R1 — Material Assembly

**Mechanic:** Wordmark forged FROM the same material as the visual world. Letterforms are sculpted from the brand's material essence (the "Subject + Material LOCK" substance carried through preceding panels).

**Material relationship:** The brand IS the substance.

**Examples:**
- T6 RUSH retest v3 — RUSH wordmark forged from lightning-current substance, plasma-arc filaments form letterforms with molten copper core inside
- Hypothetical BRAMBLE-tier RUSH — RUSH letterforms as 3D-modeled metal type charged with plasma current arcing across letterforms

**When to use:** When brand essence has a strong material identity that can be FORGED INTO letterforms (lightning, chrome, glass, liquid metal, neon, smoke).

**Pairs with:** Visual World Lock (Material LOCK substance becomes the wordmark material).

---

## R2 — Architectural Integration

**Mechanic:** Wordmark physically embossed / etched / carved INTO an architectural element. Letterforms exist as physical depressions or relief in the material surface.

**Material relationship:** The brand IS the architecture.

**Examples:**
- T4 AXIS — AXIS wordmark embossed/etched into raw concrete wall with brushed brass inlay catching skylight beam, dust motes drifting in front of carved letterforms
- BRAMBLE Panel 06 — BRAMBLE wordmark carved into wall with thorns integrating around letterforms

**When to use:** Architecture / interior design / brutalist / heritage brands where material weight matters.

**Pairs with:** Subject LOCK (architectural environment carried through preceding panels).

---

## R3 — Surface Etching

**Mechanic:** Wordmark engraved / etched / debossed onto a small surface (product / object). Letterforms exist as etched relief on a discrete material element.

**Material relationship:** The brand stamps ON the object.

**Examples:**
- Product commercial endcard — wordmark etched onto leather card / metal plate / glass slab
- Cosmetics / fragrance brand — wordmark etched onto bottle base / cap

**When to use:** Product brands with physical object essence — leather goods, watches, fragrance bottles, jewelry, premium tools.

**Pairs with:** Track B Product Commercial pipeline.

---

## R4 — Environmental Object

**Mechanic:** Wordmark appears AS a physical environmental object — a real-world signage element framed within the scene. Letterforms exist as illuminated billboard / monitor display / projected light / printed signage.

**Material relationship:** The brand IS a thing in the world.

**Examples:**
- Highway billboard endcard with wordmark + lightning bolt icon
- Storefront signage / shop window display
- Monitor UI showing wordmark as splash screen
- Wall-mounted illuminated signage in industrial space

**When to use:** Urban / lifestyle / consumer-facing brands where placement in real-world environments matters.

**Pairs with:** Tier 4 photographic cinematic register (narrow use only — when brand domain explicitly fits).

---

## R5 — Camera Reveal

**Mechanic:** Camera move reveals the wordmark from a previously occluded angle. Letterforms exist as graphic element that the camera trajectory makes visible.

**Material relationship:** The brand appears through cinematography.

**Examples:**
- Camera tilts up from product to reveal wordmark above
- Dolly pulls back to reveal wordmark + tagline on far wall
- Match-cut from product feature to wordmark on dashboard

**When to use:** When camera motion is the storytelling primary — Bot&Dolly motion-control reels, Track B product commercials with camera vocabulary.

**Pairs with:** Mode A / Mode B camera modes with explicit camera trajectory.

---

## R6 — Live Element Integration

**Mechanic:** Wordmark physically interacts with live scene elements — plant / liquid / dust / particle / light. Letterforms exist as graphic anchored with live elements interfering / interacting / passing through.

**Material relationship:** The brand interacts with the world.

**Examples:**
- T3 VERDA — wordmark with flower petal grazing 'A' and leaf curling behind 'V'
- T7 IRIS — light passes through translucent IRIS letterforms creating prismatic caustic on cyclorama, prismatic dust motes drift around letterforms
- Atmospheric closer where dust / fog / liquid touches letterforms

**When to use:** Organic / natural / living brands — botanical, beauty, wellness, food, lifestyle products with sensorial promise.

**Pairs with:** Visual World Lock natural-lock routes (Foundation / Style Ref with organic subjects).

---

## R7 — Atmospheric Flash Integration (kinetic mode)

**Mechanic:** Wordmark resolves with a single atmospheric flash / pulse / impact moment — distinct from halo glow aura (banned per Hard Rule #14.1). Letterforms exist as a kinetic resolution beat with controlled flash punctuation.

**Material relationship:** The brand impacts the scene.

**Examples:**
- Kinetic typography mode endcard — RUSH wordmark with single yellow flash on impact, no halo bloom aura
- Sports / energy brands — wordmark slams in with bass thump + brief flash burst

**When to use:** Kinetic typography (MDC6) mode where wordmark needs impact register but Hard Rule #14.1 bans halo glow / explosion burst clichés.

**Pairs with:** Hard Rule #14.1 anti-AE-template guard. Flash must be controlled (single beat, designed-render quality, NOT lens-flare slop or halo aura).

---

## Selection rules

1. Visual World Lock material identity → suggests R1 (material assembly) if material is sculptable
2. Architectural / brutalist / interior brand → suggests R2 (architectural integration)
3. Product commercial with object hero → suggests R3 (surface etching)
4. Urban / lifestyle brand with environmental placement → suggests R4 (environmental object)
5. Camera-driven cinematography reel → suggests R5 (camera reveal)
6. Organic / sensorial brand with live element interaction → suggests R6 (live element integration)
7. Kinetic typography mode without halo glow → R7 (atmospheric flash integration)

**Never mix R-types within a single reel.** Pick one and commit.

---

## Banned R-type cliché (Hard Rule #14)

- Dust burst / particle convergence brand reveal as default endcard
- Wordmark on void background (Hard Rule #14 same-template slop)
- Drama side rim light + gloss-perfection product hero shot as default
- Halo glow aura around wordmark on jet-black void (the T6 RUSH v4 sin)
- Generic "single product hero floating in dark moody workshop" endcard

Replace defaults with: R1-R7 vocabulary above with variety across reels in a batch.
