# Cinematic LED Product Reveal

A three-act, 30-second luxury product commercial. The product is the only protagonist. No on-camera human talent. All drama comes from light, atmosphere, and controlled camera movement.

---

## 1. When to use this pattern

**Best fit:**
- Premium consumer tech or lifestyle product whose physical form is visually striking
- Products with glowing, reflective, or translucent surfaces (LEDs, glass, chrome, holographic material)
- Launch campaigns targeting aspirational buyers on YouTube pre-roll, CTV, or Instagram Reels (16:9 crop)
- Brands channeling Apple, Dyson, or high-end K-drama product placement energy

**Poor fit:**
- Everyday commodity products with no visual spectacle (plain packaging, matte cardboard)
- Ads requiring a human spokesperson or testimonial credibility
- Short-form vertical (9:16) TikTok — the wide negative space and slow pacing don't compress well
- Brands that need to communicate complex features through demonstration (too abstract)

---

## 2. Hook & Opening

**Structural move: Darkness-before-dawn pattern interrupt.**

The first frame is nearly black. No product. No logo. Just atmosphere (rain, fog, void, smoke). The viewer leans in. After 8–10 seconds of pure mood, the product materializes — first as silhouette, then as light source.

**Example beat (invented, neutral product — a glowing desk speaker):**
> Scene 1 (0–10s): Drifting fog over a dark tabletop. The product is barely visible as a dark silhouette in the foreground. Camera drifts slowly forward through the haze. No voice. No text. Only music.

**Rule:** Swap the product and weather/atmosphere to match the new category. Keep the rule of *no product visibility in the first 6–8 seconds*. The product is always hidden until the atmosphere is established.

| Product category | Recommended atmosphere |
|---|---|
| Skincare / cosmetics | Soft mist, dawn light, white void |
| Audio tech (headphones) | Anechoic dark room, sound-wave ripple VFX |
| Luxury vehicle accessory | Rain-slicked asphalt, tunnel mouth |
| Glowing apparel | Fog machine, concrete underpass at night |

**Hook overlay (on-screen text, top of frame):**
- Position: y = 0.12 (top quadrant, above the subject silhouette)
- Style: minimal — white, no background, medium tracking
- Invented example: *"The Dark Was Never Empty"*
- Formula: [Antagonist noun] + [Has/Meets/Bows to] + [Implicit product promise] — do NOT name the product here.

---

## 3. Narrative Arc

**Pattern name: Atmosphere → Revelation → Coronation**

| Beat | Timing | Visual | Audio | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Atmosphere** | 0–10s | Dark establishing wide. Product as silhouette only. Slow camera drift. | Music only (no VO, no captions) | Build tension. Let viewer adjust to the darkness. Never rush this. |
| **2 — Revelation** | 10–20s | LED / light cascade races across the product surface. Slow orbit or 3/4 rotate camera. Product now lit from within. | VO begins. Problem → aggravation → product naming. | First emotional payoff. Product becomes the light source in-frame. |
| **3 — Coronation** | 20–30s | Hero push-in against pure black void (or minimal environment). Product breathing/pulsing glow. Brand wordmark fades in at ~26–27s. | VO closes with audience-identity line + brand welcome. Wordmark appears on final beat. | Brand lock. Product alone on stage. Name lands on silence. |

**Target VO word counts:**
- Scene 2: ~20–24 words (≤ 2.4 words/second)
- Scene 3: ~14–18 words (≤ 2.2 words/second)
- Scene 1: **zero words** — silence + music only, no exceptions

**VO rhythm pattern (Scene 2 — invented example):**
> "The quiet has always been flat, dull, and forgettable. Until now. Meet the [PRODUCT] that doesn't just play sound — it fills the room."

Structure: [Category pain point, 3-beat list] → [pivot phrase] → [Meet the X] → [reframed brand promise]

**VO rhythm pattern (Scene 3 — invented example):**
> "Made for those who listen closer, feel deeper, live louder. Welcome to the future of sound."

Structure: [Identity line with anaphora] → [Category-wide welcome statement] — The brand name is spoken here OR the wordmark handles it (not both).

## 4. Visual Style Spec

**Aesthetic:** Apple product film × K-drama cinematography. Every frame should be frameable.

| Dimension | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Format** | 16:9, 1920×1080 minimum |
| **Palette** | Near-black background (#0a0a0f or cooler). Product illumination: blue-white LED, gold filament, or brand color glow. No warm orange. |
| **Camera grammar** | Scene 1: Locked-off or barely perceptible forward drift. Scene 2: Slow orbit (10–15° arc over 10s). Scene 3: Slow push-in on hero. No handheld. No fast pan. |
| **Shot length** | No cut under 5 seconds. This is a three-shot film. Rhythm lives in camera movement, not editing. |
| **Slow motion** | Scene 1 and 2 should render at or imply slow-motion (rain, particles, fabric, liquid). Scene 3 is real-time. |
| **Lighting model** | Product is the primary light source in-scene. Practical light only — no visible light stands. Volumetric rays acceptable in Scene 2. |
| **On-screen text** | Hook overlay: top frame, Scene 1. Captions: word-by-word, editorial-clean preset (Montserrat or similar geometric sans, sentence case). Scene 1: mute captions entirely. Wordmark: Scene 3, bottom-center, fade in at ~6.5s into the scene, heavyweight (800–900 weight), brand color or white + brand glow text-shadow. |
| **Music volume** | 0.25 (elevated slightly vs default 0.2 — dramatic orchestral needs room to breathe under VO) |

## 5. Voice & Persona

**Setup:** Voice-only persona (no on-camera face). Middle-aged male, calm-authoritative timbre. Think: luxury automotive narrator crossed with prestige fragrance ad. NOT hype. NOT conversational. NOT friendly.

**Tone anchors:**
- Deliberate. Leaves space between phrases.
- Conspiratorial intimacy — speaks as if revealing a secret the viewer deserves to know.
- Never shouty, never salesy.

**Persona archetype:** *The Knowing Narrator* — omniscient, slightly detached, certain. The product is beneath them to sell; they are simply announcing something inevitable.

**Phrases that fit this voice:**
- "Until now." (pivot marker — very effective at 1/3 mark of VO)
- "Those who [identity verb] differently." (anaphora identity construction)
- "Welcome to [category] + [superlative]." (coronation close)
- "It doesn't just [category function] — it [elevated reframe]." (reframe structure)

**Phrases to avoid:**
- "Check out / you'll love / amazing / game-changer" (too conversational)
- Second-person direct address before Scene 3 (earn it)
- Any pricing, urgency, or scarcity language (breaks the luxury register)

---

## 6. CTA Mechanic

**Pattern: Soft identity-close + wordmark landing.**

There is no explicit "click here" or "link in bio" CTA. The close is aspirational identity affiliation — viewer wants to belong to the world of the product.

- **Spoken CTA (Scene 3 VO):** "Welcome to the future of [category]." — The brand name is either spoken here OR appears as wordmark (not both, to avoid redundancy).
- **On-screen wordmark:** Fades in at the final beat of Scene 3. Bottom-center. Heavyweight type. Brand color or white. Optional: subtle glow text-shadow matching product LED color.
- **No overlay cards, no URL bars, no price tags** in the 30s cut. These can be appended as a 5s card in the platform-specific export.

---

## 7. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

Treat these as non-negotiable:

1. **Opaque product body.** If the product has a surface, it must render as opaque/solid — not translucent, glass-like, or wireframe — unless the product's physical design is explicitly transparent. The LED glow comes *from the edges/seams/details*, not through the body. Watch for first-pass image renders that come back translucent when they should be solid.

2. **Scene 1 is always silence.** No voice-over. No captions. Music only. Muting captions for this scene is mandatory in the assemble step.

3. **Wordmark goes in final assembly, not in director payload.** The brand name text overlay is handled at the `assemble_video` level as a `text_overlay`, not described inside the director's scene style_direction. This prevents double-rendering and keeps the wordmark composited cleanly over the final render.

4. **VO pacing cap: 2.4 words/second.** Luxury narration breathes. Write shorter, speak slower. If a VO line tests over 2.4 w/s, cut words — do not speed up delivery.

5. **Hook overlay position: y=0.12.** Top of frame. The scene subject is in center-to-lower frame; the hook sits above it in dark negative space. Do not center the hook overlay vertically.

6. **Music volume 0.25** for dramatic orchestral scores. Default 0.2 is too quiet for this aesthetic; 0.3+ drowns the VO.

7. **No warm color palette.** This pattern lives in cool/neutral darkness. Amber, orange, or warm-white lighting breaks the aesthetic and reads as "rustic" or "cozy" — incompatible with luxury tech positioning.
