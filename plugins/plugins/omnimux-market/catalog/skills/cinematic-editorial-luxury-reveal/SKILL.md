# Cinematic Editorial Luxury Reveal

A four-beat ad pattern for fashion accessories and premium lifestyle products. The structure earns attention with silence and a typographic gut-punch, builds desire through a confident on-camera persona, widens the product story with color/variant options, and converts with a clean spoken + on-screen CTA.

---

## 1. When to use this pattern

**Best fit:**
- Fashion accessories: handbags, jewelry, shoes, belts, scarves
- Beauty / fragrance products with a premium positioning
- Lifestyle home goods with visual variety (color/material options)
- Price points that need to feel elevated: roughly $30–$300
- YouTube 16:9 primary; adapts to Instagram Reels 9:16 with minor reframing
- Audiences: women 25–40, interest clusters around fashion, self-expression, everyday luxury

**Poor fit:**
- Commodity / utility products (power tools, supplements, bulk goods)
- Brands where UGC rawness is the trust signal (streetwear, budget fast fashion)
- Products with no visual variety or only one colorway — the Color Showcase beat loses impact
- Very short placements (under 20 s) — the four-beat structure needs room to breathe

---

## 2. Hook & Opening

**Structural move: Silent Macro Hero + Typographic Statement**

Open with a close-up product reveal — no music, no voiceover, pure visual — then detonate a two-line serif hook over the product.

**Generic example (invented, for a neutral hypothetical product):**
> "The Mug That Knows  
> Your Morning"

**The move:** The silence creates a micro-pause that stops the scroll. The typographic statement does the brand work without a word of spoken copy. The hook is never a product feature — it is a statement about *identity*, *status*, or *what the product does for the person who carries it*.

**Swap rule:** Replace the product noun and the identity claim. Keep the structure — two lines, first line names the product (obliquely), second line names the social/emotional payoff. Keep the silence.

**Hook writing formula:**
| Slot | Example | Swap guidance |
|------|---------|---------------|
| Line 1 | "The Mug That Knows" | "The [Product] That [Verb]s" — something the product *does* socially |
| Line 2 | "Your Morning" | A time-, contrast-, or identity-completing phrase |

**Typography lock:** Serif display face, minimal overlay style, centered, no drop shadow, white on dark or product-toned background.

**Duration:** 5–7 s. No voiceover. Music enters at scene 2, not here.

---

## 3. Narrative Arc

Total target runtime: 28–35 s.

| Beat | Duration | Name | Visual mode | Audio |
|------|----------|------|-------------|-------|
| 1 | 5–7 s | Silent Macro Hero | Product only, macro/close-up, no persona | Silent — hook text overlay only |
| 2 | 8–12 s | Persona Presentation | On-camera, persona speaking directly to lens | Sync sound + music under (low vol) |
| 3 | 7–10 s | Color / Variant Showcase | Persona voice-over; product variants cycle on screen | Voice-over + music |
| 4 | 6–8 s | Dark Shimmer CTA | Abstract background, no product or persona in frame | Voice-over + music; on-screen text |

**Beat-by-beat copy shape:**

- **Beat 1:** No spoken copy. Hook text only.
- **Beat 2 (Persona Presentation):** Persona introduces the product naturally — holds it, demonstrates a key feature (e.g. handle, lid, interior), and delivers one desire-building line. Tone: warm, peer-to-peer, not pitchy. Example rhythm: *"I found this at [brand] and I honestly couldn't believe the price. The [key feature] — [payoff phrase]."*
- **Beat 3 (Color Showcase):** Persona voice continues off-camera. Enumerate color/material options conversationally. Name 3–5 variants maximum. End with an inclusivity or versatility statement. Example: *"It comes in [color A], [color B], [color C] — something for every vibe."*
- **Beat 4 (CTA):** Single clear action. No more than three elements on-screen. Spoken line mirrors or echoes the on-screen text. Example: *"Only [price]. [URL]. [Tagline]."*

---

## 4. Visual Style Spec

**Overall register:** Cinematic editorial — NOT UGC. Everything is composed, lit, and graded. Handheld is fine if it reads as *intentional* movement, not shaky authenticity.

| Element | Spec |
|---------|------|
| Aspect ratio | 16:9, 1920×1080 (YouTube primary) |
| Lens feel | 85 mm equivalent, shallow DoF — product and persona are sharp, backgrounds melt |
| Color palette | Warm champagne highlights, charcoal/deep-burgundy shadows — no harsh whites, no neon |
| Lighting | Soft directional light (window light or beauty dish style); no flat ring-light look |
| Setting | Dressing room, vanity, boutique dressing area, or soft-lit bedroom — intimate, personal |
| Camera grammar | Beat 1: locked-off macro. Beat 2: slight push-in or gentle handheld drift. Beat 3: product close-ups cut on rhythm. Beat 4: locked-off, abstract shimmer BG |
| Shot length | Beat 1: single long take (5–7 s). Beat 2: 2–3 cuts. Beat 3: cut per color (1.5–2 s per variant). Beat 4: single locked-off take |
| On-screen text | Serif display face throughout. Beat 1: hook overlay minimal. Beat 2–3: editorial-clean captions (auto from speech). Beat 4: manual text overlays only (price / URL / tagline) |
| End card BG | Dark abstract shimmer — no product, no persona; let text breathe |

---

## 5. Voice & Persona

**Persona archetype:** Aspirational peer — late 20s to mid-30s, dressed one step above "everyday" (satin, tailored trousers, layered gold jewelry). She looks like someone the target audience *wants to be*, not a celebrity they can't access.

**Tone:** Warm, calm-authoritative. Not hyped. Not hushed ASMR. Conversational but composed — like she's sharing a discovery with a close friend.

**Pacing:** Measured. Pauses are allowed. No rush-to-fill-silence energy. Let a beat land before moving to the next.

**Vocal style anchors:**
- Confident product hold — she *uses* the product physically (open lid, show interior, switch hand to handle)
- Self-directed discovery framing: "I found…", "What I love is…", "It comes in…"
- No superlatives ("the best", "amazing") — opt for specific sensory or functional detail instead

**Persona visual lock (swap the specifics, keep the register):**
- Complexion: any — do not default to any single ethnicity; match brand audience diversity
- Wardrobe: one elevated piece (satin, silk, structured blazer) + simple base; color must complement product palette
- Setting: always personal/intimate (never a white studio unless brand demands it)

---

## 6. CTA Mechanic

**Pattern: Triple Stack End Card (price / URL / tagline)**

Three on-screen text elements on a dark shimmer background. Spoken voice-over reads them in sequence — price first (value anchor), URL second (action), tagline last (emotional close).

**Generic CTA example (invented):**
> On-screen + spoken: "Only $48 / theproduct.com / Make Every Morning Yours"

**Swap rule:** Keep the three-element stack. The price must come first — leading with value before the URL removes the friction of "is this even affordable?" before asking for a click. Tagline should echo the hook's identity theme.

**Mechanic type:** Direct response (link in bio / URL on screen). No comment-bait. No soft sign-off. The spoken CTA and on-screen text are redundant by design — reinforce each other.

---

## 7. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

These are the creative constraints locked for this pattern. Any future use of this skill must honor them:

1. **Beat 1 is always silent.** No music sting, no voice. The silence is the attention hook. Music enters at Beat 2 onset only.
2. **Hook text uses a serif display face.** Do not substitute sans-serif or a kinetic display face. The serif carries the editorial/feminine/luxury register.
3. **Caption preset is editorial-clean.** Do not use bold kinetic captions or auto-highlight styles — they break the tone.
4. **Kling requires a frontal headshot persona.** If the persona was not set up with frontal framing, kling will fail and seedance is the correct fallback. Do not force kling on a candid-framed persona.
5. **Music volume is low (≤ 0.12).** The ad's emotional weight comes from the voice and visuals, not the score. Music is atmosphere, not foreground.
6. **Beat 4 end card has no product or persona in frame.** The dark shimmer background is intentional — it isolates the CTA text and prevents visual competition.
7. **Product variants are shown in Beat 3, not Beat 2.** Beat 2 is a single-product moment (the hero SKU). Beat 3 is discovery. Do not conflate them.
8. **Tagline echoes the hook's identity theme.** If the hook is about presence or routine, the tagline closes the loop on that same theme. They must rhyme thematically.
9. **Persona wardrobe must complement, not match, the product palette.** A "dressed up" wardrobe tone with a metallic or jewel-tone product works because the wardrobe reads as elevated while the product still has room to shine. Avoid same-hue matching — the product should win the eye.
10. **Total runtime: 28–35 s.** Under 28 s, Beat 3 (color showcase) feels rushed. Over 35 s, YouTube audience drop-off erodes the CTA.
