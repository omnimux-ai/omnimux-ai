# Cinematic Service Brand Reveal

A premium three-act arc that turns a local service business into an aspirational brand. The **transformed environment or result** is always the hero — not the provider. Brand name, service category, location, colors, and contact details are fully swappable. Structure, pacing, visual grammar, and voice archetype stay locked.

---

## 1. When to use this pattern

**Best fit:**
- Local/regional service businesses with strong visual outcomes: interior restoration, landscaping, cleaning, salon/beauty, photography, architecture, real estate staging, office fit-out
- Brands projecting premium or trust positioning — not discount/price-war messaging
- Short-form vertical video (Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts) — 9:16, 28–33 seconds
- Target audiences: homeowners, property managers, small business owners who care about aesthetic quality and reliability

**Poor fit:**
- Pure e-commerce / physical product unboxing (use a product-reveal arc instead)
- High-energy discount promos needing rapid cuts and hype (use a rapid-cut hook arc)
- B2B enterprise with no visible deliverable or transformation

---

## 2. Hook & Opening (Seconds 0–10)

**Structural move:** Aspirational slow reveal — open on the *result*, not the process. The viewer sees beauty first; the brand earns the right to explain later.

**Hook archetype:** Quiet aspiration statement. A declarative sentence that names the viewer's latent desire. Not a question, not a stat shock, not a pattern interrupt.

**Generic hook example (invented, for a hypothetical service):**
> *"Your space deserves to be transformed."*

**Hook overlay style:** Two-line split, centered on frame. Clean sans-serif (Montserrat or equivalent), minimal/light weight. NOT kinetic or loud — the visual must breathe around the text.

**Swap rule:** Replace the domain noun (space → garden, skin, office) and the transformation verb (transformed → renewed, revitalized, reimagined). Keep the exact sentence shape: `[Possessive] [domain noun] [verb of deserving] [transformation].` Examples:
- "Your garden deserves to bloom." (landscaping)
- "Your skin deserves the best care." (skincare salon)
- "Your office deserves to be reimagined." (office design)

**Visual direction for Beat 1:**
- Cinematic slow push-in or slow reveal on the finished, high-quality result
- Locked-off or ultra-gentle movement — zero handheld shake
- Dark, moody grade: navy/charcoal shadows, warm accent light (gold, orange, amber)
- No people in the first 3–4 seconds — let the result breathe
- VO starts at approximately second 2, unhurried pace

---

## 3. Narrative Arc

| Beat | Timing | Name | What happens | Visual tone | VO tone |
|------|--------|------|--------------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | 0–10s | **Slow Reveal / Hook** | Aspirational open on the result; hook overlay appears; narrator states the desire | Cinematic, still, moody | Calm, warm, measured |
| 2 | 10–20s | **Proof in Action** | Professionals at work; services listed as on-screen checklist in accent color; 2–3 cuts across work contexts | Dynamic but controlled | Confident, factual, trust-building |
| 3 | 20–30s | **Brand Card / CTA** | Logo on dark premium background; location tag; phone number in accent color; CTA button | Static premium card | Soft close — free quote / contact offer |

**Arc shape:** Aspiration → Credibility → Accessibility.
**Emotional journey:** "I want that" → "These people can deliver it" → "I can reach them easily."

---

## 4. Visual Style Spec

- **Format:** 9:16 vertical, 1080×1920
- **Duration:** 28–33 seconds
- **Style:** Cinematic premium — NOT UGC, no selfie cam, no raw phone footage
- **Color palette:** Dark anchor colors (navy, charcoal, deep teal) + warm accent (gold, orange, amber). Light is directional and controlled.
- **Camera grammar:**
  - Beat 1: Locked-off or ultra-slow push-in. Zero shake.
  - Beat 2: Gentle handheld or slow pan — conveys energy without chaos. ~1 cut per 3–4 seconds.
  - Beat 3: Completely static brand card.
- **Shot length distribution:** Slow (Beat 1) → moderate (Beat 2) → static (Beat 3). No rapid cuts anywhere in the video.
- **On-screen text policy:**
  - Beat 1: Hook overlay only — 2 lines, centered, minimal weight
  - Beat 2: Services checklist in brand accent color (orange/gold), left or center aligned, appears line by line as VO names them
  - Beat 3: Brand logo (top third), location tag (center), phone number in accent color (large — screenshottable), CTA button (green or brand color, pill shape)
- **Caption style:** Clean-bold, word-by-word reveal, Montserrat. Present throughout for silent autoplay.

---

## 5. Voice & Persona

- **Persona archetype:** Calm authority narrator — NOT salesperson, NOT hype voice
- **Gender/age:** Male, middle-aged as default — projects reliability and expertise. Adjust per brand identity only if explicitly requested.
- **Language:** Match the brand's local language in full — every overlay, VO line, caption, and CTA button uses the same language. Never mix languages within one video.
- **Tone:** Warm, unhurried, slightly understated. Never shouts. Invites rather than pressures.
- **Pacing:** Deliberate — allow visual pauses. VO need not fill every second; silence over Beat 1 visuals is welcome.
- **Sample phrasing rhythm (template):**
  - Beat 1: "[Declarative desire statement]. [Brand name] [brief brand promise]."
  - Beat 2: "[Services listed conversationally, not bullet-read]. [Guarantee or trust signal]."
  - Beat 3: "[Location]. [CTA — free quote, contact us]. [Brand name close or tagline]."
- **Hard rule:** VO should sound like a trusted neighbor recommending a service — not an ad read. No superlatives ("best in the world"), no urgency pressure ("call NOW before it's too late").

---

## 6. CTA Mechanic

**Type:** Soft-close with contact facilitation. Not comment-bait. Not link-in-bio-only.

**Structure for Beat 3 (last ~10 seconds):**
1. **Location tag** visible on screen — city + region (e.g., "[City] and surrounding area")
2. **Phone number** displayed prominently in accent color — font large enough to screenshot from a phone
3. **CTA button overlay** — short action text on a pill/rounded-rect button: "Request a Quote" / "Contact Us" / "Book Now" / equivalent in local language
4. **VO mirrors** the on-screen offer: "Get in touch and request your free quote."

**Core principle:** The CTA removes friction. The viewer can act directly from the video without navigating anywhere. The phone number is the primary conversion asset.

**Swapping the CTA for other brands:** Replace phone with WhatsApp link text, DM prompt, or booking URL as needed. Maintain the three-layer structure: location → contact → action.

---

## 7. Background Music

- **Style:** Instrumental — elegant, corporate, restrained. Piano + strings or light orchestral. No beats, no EDM, no lo-fi drums.
- **Mood:** Aspirational but calm. Premium brand video feel, not motivational montage.
- **Duration:** Generate ~2 seconds longer than the final video to allow a clean fade-out.
- **Volume mix:** 0.10–0.15. VO and visuals are the heroes; music is atmosphere only.
- **Hard rule:** Music must never overpower VO. If uncertain, go lower.

---

## 8. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

Treat each as a non-negotiable constraint.

1. **Phone number must appear on screen.** For any brand using this pattern, the phone number (or primary contact) MUST be a visible on-screen element in Beat 3 — not spoken-only in VO. Re-assemble the video if needed to add it as a visible overlay.

2. **All text, overlays, captions, and UI elements must be in the brand's language.** Every piece of on-screen text uses the brand's local language. Do not mix with English, even for "standard" labels.

3. **Music volume hard cap: 0.15.** Do not go higher even for a "more premium" feel. VO clarity is the primary trust signal.

4. **No rapid cuts.** This pattern depends entirely on its slow, premium editing rhythm. A rapid-cut pass destroys the brand positioning. Minimum 3 seconds per shot; Beat 1 shots can run 5–7 seconds.

5. **Services checklist uses accent color, not white.** VO caption subtitles can be white. The services overlay list must be in brand accent (orange, gold, or equivalent) to visually differentiate it.

6. **Beat 3 brand card is static.** Subtle background texture or gentle particle atmosphere is acceptable; the card must not animate aggressively. The logo should feel planted and confident.

7. **Always include geographic specificity.** The city and region name belongs on screen in Beat 3 — a real "[City] and surrounding area" tag is more trustworthy than a generic tagline. This is a local-service trust signal, not filler.
