# Meme-Merch Multi-Format TikTok

## 1 — When to use this pattern

**Best fit:**
- Novelty, meme, or slogan apparel (t-shirts, hoodies, hats) sold DTC via TikTok Shop or link-in-bio
- Brands that want social proof ("real person owns this") plus aspirational ("this looks *good*") in the same campaign
- 2–4 product SKUs that can each carry one format; or one hero SKU across all three formats
- Audiences 16–34 who respond to irony, identity signaling, and deadpan humor

**Poor fit:**
- Luxury or high-fashion positioning (the UGC rawness undercuts prestige)
- Products that need extensive feature explanation (the formats are vibe-first, not demo-first)
- Brands that cannot supply flat-lay product images as reference (garment fidelity suffers)

---

## 2 — Hook & Opening

**Archetype: POV identity claim + visual confirmation**

The hook is a first-person declaration of identity that the *shirt is the punchline for*. The viewer hears a bold, slightly absurd self-description, then the camera confirms it by revealing the shirt. The joke lands because the shirt says what the person just implied.

**Invented generic example (fit-check UGC):**
> "POV: you found the mug that explains your entire morning routine."

**Structural move:** Statement of recognition → product as proof. The viewer thinks "that's me" before they see the shirt; seeing the shirt closes the loop.

**Swap rule:** Keep the "POV: You just found the [X] that [identity statement]" frame. Replace [X] with the product category and [identity statement] with the meme/slogan premise on the shirt. Never swap to a feature-first hook ("This shirt is made of…") — that kills the identity-claim mechanic.

**For unboxing format:** Hook is anticipation-build: creator holds sealed package, says nothing or says one teasing line ("okay wait—"), then cuts hard to reveal. Silence + physical action does the hook work.

**For cinematic format:** No spoken hook. The hook is purely visual — first frame is a close crop of the shirt's graphic (macro, high contrast) before pulling to reveal the full look. The graphic does the identity-claim work without dialogue.

## 3 — Narrative Arc

### Format A — UGC Fit-Check (≈17s, 3 scenes)

| Beat | Target duration | Voice/Visual tone |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** — POV identity claim, creator in frame, shirt visible | 0–4s | Casual, direct to camera, slightly conspiratorial. Handheld or static medium shot. |
| **Body** — "this one says [shirt text]" + reaction/styling moment | 4–12s | Playful, a little deadpan. Cut on movement. Show graphic clearly. |
| **CTA** — comment-to-DM close | 12–17s | Warm, lower energy than body. Creator looks at camera, soft smile. |

### Format B — UGC Unboxing (≈15s, single clip 4 hard cuts)

| Beat | Label | Target duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Packed** | PACKED | 0–3s | Package in frame, hands fidgeting. Build anticipation silently. |
| **Reveal** | REVEAL | 3–7s | Shirt pulled out, creator's face reacts. Fast cut IN on reaction. |
| **Product focus** | PRODUCT-FOCUS | 7–11s | Camera drifts to graphic on shirt. Hold. Let the joke land. |
| **Satisfaction** | SATISFACTION | 11–15s | Creator holds shirt up, nods/laughs. Implicit approval. |

### Format C — Cinematic Fit-Check (≈15s, single clip 4 hard cuts)

| Beat | Label | Target duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-wear** | PRE_WEAR | 0–3s | Macro crop of shirt graphic, shallow DOF. Cinematic grade. No movement. |
| **Wearing** | WEARING | 3–7s | Full-body or 3/4 shot, styled look. Locked-off or very slow push. |
| **Texture closeup** | TEXTURE_CLOSEUP | 7–11s | Fabric/graphic detail. Single source key light, high contrast. |
| **Style pose** | STYLE_POSE | 11–15s | Final frame, editorial. Creator not smiling — cool, composed. |

## 4 — Visual Style Spec

### UGC formats (A & B)
- **Aesthetic:** Authentic bedroom/apartment. Not staged. Natural window light or ring-light-soft. No color grading beyond mild warmth.
- **Camera:** Handheld feel (slight wobble acceptable). Selfie angle or slight below-eye-level for fit-check. Above-counter angle for unboxing.
- **Shot length:** Fast — 1.5–3s per cut. Rhythm accelerates through the body beat.
- **On-screen text:** Frenzy-style captions — bold, slightly chaotic placement, color-matched to shirt's dominant graphic color. Never subtitle every word; hit the punchline words only.
- **Color:** Warm, slightly saturated. Skin tones natural. Shirt colors accurate to product reference.

### Cinematic format (C)
- **Aesthetic:** Editorial. Controlled lighting. Pick one cohesive room-plus-key-light palette (e.g., a muted wall tone with a single colored side-key) and a matching high-key grade.
- **Camera:** TRIPOD ONLY for all cuts (hard rule — see §7). No handheld, no gimbal drift language in prompts.
- **Lens language:** 50mm anamorphic equivalent. Three depth layers (foreground element, subject, background bokeh).
- **Shot length:** Slower — 2.5–4s per cut. Rhythm is deliberate and cool.
- **On-screen text:** Editorial-clean lower-thirds or none. Never frenzy captions on cinematic format.
- **Color grade:** A single high-key tinted grade with rich shadows. Shirt color should pop against the grade — if the shirt is black, the grade handles all the visual interest.

### Persona anchors (keep locked across all formats)
- One distinctive, memorable creator look (a signature haircut/color, a clear style identity, a consistent streetwear aesthetic). Define it once and reuse the exact same description everywhere.
- One consistent setting — a single bedroom/room palette is the through-line that makes all three formats feel like one creator's account.
- Voice IDs are reused across formats verbatim (do not regenerate persona between formats).

## 5 — Voice & Persona

**Archetype:** Peer reviewer — not a hype-beast, not an influencer performing enthusiasm. Someone who found something genuinely funny/relatable and is sharing it without overselling.

**Tone:** Deadpan-warm. The joke is in the shirt, not in the delivery. Creator's voice stays relatively flat; the irony of the shirt does the comedic lifting.

**Pacing:** Moderate. Slight pause before the shirt's text is read aloud — let the viewer read it themselves first, then the creator says it like a punchline confirmation.

**Sample phrasing patterns:**
- "okay so this one says [shirt text] and I just—" [trail off, let viewer fill in the reaction]
- "POV: you are [identity the shirt encodes]"
- "if this is you, you need this" [no explanation of why]

**What NOT to do:** Do not write copy that explains the joke. If the shirt's slogan is a punchline on its own, the voiceover does not spell out the deeper meaning behind it. Trust the shirt. Trust the audience.

---

## 6 — CTA Mechanic

**UGC Fit-Check:** Comment-to-DM. Spoken + on-screen. Exact wording template:
> "Comment [WORD] and I'll DM you the link 👇"

[WORD] should be the shirt's meme word/phrase (a single short keyword from the slogan). This turns the CTA into a second engagement with the shirt's joke — the viewer has to say the meme to get the link.

**Unboxing:** Soft sign-off. No hard CTA. End on satisfaction beat; caption card with shop link or "link in bio." The format is awareness/desire, not conversion-primary.

**Cinematic Fit-Check:** On-screen card only. No spoken CTA. One line of text at the final frame: brand name + "shop now" or just the product name. Speaking a CTA over cinematic footage breaks the editorial feel.

## 7 — Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

Treat each of these as inviolable.

1. **Single persona across all formats.** Call `setup_persona` (via `ads-persona-prompt-skill`) once, save the persona reference, and reuse for every subsequent format. Do not regenerate or create a second persona "for variety."

2. **TRIPOD hard rule for cinematic format.** The cinematic fit-check uses locked-off shots only. Do not include "slow push," "handheld," "gimbal," or any camera movement language in motion prompts for the TRIPOD cuts. Static = cinematic here.

3. **Product images as PRODUCT REFERENCE, not style reference.** Flat-lay product images are passed to director/board generation as garment fidelity anchors. They are not used to dictate the video's color palette or visual style.

4. **Comment-to-DM CTA (not "link in bio") for UGC fit-check.** Use comment-bait as the mechanic for the fit-check format; reserve "link in bio" for the unboxing sign-off.

5. **Music separation by format.** UGC formats use quirky indie-pop (`music:bg`). Cinematic format uses dreamy lo-fi (`music:cinematic`). Do not use cinematic music on UGC formats — it signals high-production and undermines the authenticity.

6. **Ads-persona-prompt-skill before setup_persona.** Always load the persona prompt skill first to avoid LLM default persona archetypes (generic influencer, stock-photo energy).

7. **ads-cinematic-skill vocabulary injected into cinematic board prompts.** When producing the cinematic fit-check, load `ads-cinematic-skill` and explicitly inject its vocabulary (anamorphic, depth layers, color grade spec) into both the storyboard prompt and the motion prompt. Loading the skill without injection leaves the vocabulary unused.

8. **Reference the existing persona by final:video output when chaining formats.** When starting a second or third format, pass the `final:video` from the first format as persona reference to ensure continuity — don't rely on text description alone.
