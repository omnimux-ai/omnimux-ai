# Spanish UGC Community Spokesperson Hook

A 26-second (±3s) TikTok / Reels pattern for high-trust, considered-purchase products targeting Spanish-speaking audiences. The creative engine is a single trusted community insider — a mid-50s professional woman — who opens with a fear-confronting rhetorical question, validates the viewer's lived experience, presents the product as the obvious community-approved solution, and closes with a phone CTA.

---

## 1. When to Use This Pattern

**Best fit:**
- High-trust services aimed at Spanish-speaking audiences (financial protection, coverage plans, health, household services)
- Products where trust and community identification are the primary purchase barriers
- Categories where a phone call is the natural conversion action
- TikTok, Instagram Reels, Facebook short-form video

**Poor fit:**
- Bilingual or English-primary audiences (use an English UGC pattern instead)
- Youth-skewing products (persona archetype won't land with Gen Z)
- E-commerce impulse buys (hook arc is too slow/emotional for impulse categories)
- Luxury or aspirational products (tone is peer/community, not aspirational)

---

## 2. Hook & Opening

**Structural move:** *Rhetorical fear-confrontation question* — opens mid-sentence, on-camera, direct eye contact, no title card, no music swell. The question names a fear the viewer has already felt but hasn't articulated. It does not ask about the product; it asks about the viewer's life.

**Invented generic example (for a neutral home-services product):**
> "¿Qué va a hacer usted cuando algo se descomponga en plena noche?"
> *(What are you going to do when something breaks down in the middle of the night?)*

**The move:** name the feared outcome, not the product. The breakdown scenario is the fear. The product is the answer — but it doesn't appear in the hook.

**Swap rule:** Replace the fear object (a midnight breakdown → an unexpected bill, a debt burden, etc.) to match the new product's core anxiety. Keep the grammatical structure: *¿Quién / Qué / Cómo [verb] cuando [feared scenario]?* Never swap the question format for a statement. The question creates a micro-pause of self-identification in the viewer's scroll behavior.

**Hook overlay spec (hard rule):** White text, outlined/drop-shadow style, placed in the **top zone** of frame (not bottom — bottom is obscured by TikTok UI). Font weight: bold. No animation on entry; text is static and readable within the first 0.5s.

---

## 3. Narrative Arc

**Pattern name:** *Fear → Empathy → Solution → Phone*

| Beat | Duration | Content | Tone |
|------|----------|---------|------|
| **Hook** | 0–5s | Rhetorical question naming the core fear. Spokesperson on camera, direct eye contact. No cutaways. | Warm, direct, slightly grave |
| **Empathy** | 5–12s | Validate the fear with a lived-experience scenario (e.g., "Many families face an expense they didn't see coming"). Name the community, not a statistic. | Soft, peer-level, nodding |
| **Solution** | 12–20s | Introduce the product as the community-proven answer. Lead with accessibility markers: no complicated process, low monthly cost (anchor with specific $ figure), fast setup. | Confident, reassuring, slightly proud |
| **CTA** | 20–26s | Single clear action. Phone number on screen. Spoken + displayed simultaneously. Brand URL displayed as secondary. | Warm urgency — "protect your family today" framing |

**Do not add a fifth beat.** The 26s arc is intentionally tight. Every extra beat bleeds watch-time.

---

## 4. Visual Style Spec

**Format:** UGC handheld — not cinematic. Slight camera movement (breathing, micro-shake) signals authenticity. Do not over-stabilize.

**Setting:** Warm, lived-in home interior. **Family photos or bookshelves visible in background** as mood prop — signals "this person has a family; she understands your life." Not a studio. Not a kitchen.

**Wardrobe:** Conservative, professional, community-appropriate:
- High-trust/financial-style categories: fully buttoned blouse (navy, burgundy, or deep teal preferred), gold accent jewelry (small earrings, thin necklace). No logos. No casual wear.
- The "fully buttoned, professional" rule is non-negotiable for high-trust categories — it reads as trustworthy in this audience.

**Color palette:** Warm neutral walls, soft natural window light. No harsh ring-light look. No neon. Skin tone should read true — no heavy color grade.

**Camera grammar:**
- Medium close-up (chest to top of head) for hook and empathy beats
- Slight push-in permissible on solution beat to signal confidence
- No jump cuts in the first 5s — hold the hook shot

**On-screen text policy:**
- Hook: outlined/drop-shadow overlay, top zone, static
- Captions: soft-pill style (e.g., Poppins), **word-by-word** Spanish, bottom zone — critical for sound-off viewing
- CTA scene: **mute captions** on the CTA scene; replace with explicit CTA pill (phone number + brand URL). Do not let auto-captions compete with the phone number display.
- Legal disclaimer: fine-print text on the **closing scene** (see Hard Rules below)

**Shot length distribution:** Hook shot holds 5s. Empathy 1–2 cuts max. Solution 2–3 cuts. CTA holds 6s. No rapid cutting — this demographic responds to deliberate pacing.

**Music:** Soft Latin acoustic instrumental (guitar preferred). Mixed at **≤10% volume** — present but never competes with voice. Mood: warm, familiar, non-threatening.

---

## 5. Voice & Persona

**Persona archetype:** *Trusted Community Insider* — not an authority figure from above, not a peer from below. A mid-50s woman who has navigated the same system and come out informed. She speaks to you like a knowledgeable family friend.

**Demographics anchor:** Mid-50s, professional woman. For high-trust categories: navy blouse, gold-rimmed glasses, warm living room with family-photo bookshelf. These are anchors — swap wardrobe color and setting for other products, keep the demographic and professionalism.

**Tone:** Warm authority. Not sales-y. Not hyped. Not hushed/ASMR. Conversational but deliberate — every sentence lands. Think: the knowledgeable family friend who works in the field and actually explains things.

**Speech pacing:** 2.2–2.5 words per second. Slower than English UGC norms. This pacing signals respect and allows processing. Do not exceed 2.8 w/s.

**Language:** 100% Spanish. No Spanglish. No code-switching mid-sentence. Use a natural, conversational Latin American register (avoid Castilian vosotros forms). Use "usted" (formal you) — this audience responds to respect, not casual familiarity.

**Sample phrasing patterns (invented generic examples):**
- Hook: *"¿[Fear question]?"* — rhetorical, pause after
- Empathy: *"Muchas familias… [shared scenario]"* — inclusive framing
- Solution: *"Por eso existe [product] — sin complicaciones, desde $[X] al mes"* — accessibility first
- CTA: *"Llame ahora y proteja a su familia"* — action + emotional anchor

---

## 6. CTA Mechanic

**Mechanic: Phone call.** This is the required CTA for high-trust services targeting this demographic. Do NOT use:
- "Link in bio" — this audience does not navigate to bio links for high-trust purchases
- "Swipe up" — outdated
- "DM us" — too casual for high-trust products

**Execution:**
- Spoken CTA: *"Llame ahora al [phone number]"* — spoken by spokesperson on camera
- On-screen CTA pill: phone number displayed large, brand URL displayed smaller below it
- Both displayed simultaneously with spoken line
- Hold the CTA frame for the full 6s closing beat
- Captions muted on this scene (so the phone number is the only text on screen)

**Wording anchor (invented generic example):** *"Llame ahora y proteja a su familia"* — the "protect your family" emotional close after the phone number is the warmth valve. Do not end cold on just the number.

---

## 7. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

These are locked decisions for this pattern. Treat each as a hard constraint.

1. **Phone CTA is mandatory for high-trust categories.** Link-in-bio is wrong for these categories and this audience. Never substitute it.
2. **Legal disclaimer fine print on closing scene.** Regulated categories require disclosures. Always place a fine-print disclaimer text overlay on the final scene. Do not omit even if the brand doesn't explicitly request it.
3. **Captions muted on CTA scene.** Auto-captions will crowd the phone number. Always set `mute_captions: true` on the CTA/closing scene and use a dedicated CTA pill overlay instead.
4. **Wardrobe is fully buttoned/professional.** For high-trust categories, no casual wear, no open collars, no athleisure. Conservative professional is a trust signal for this demographic.
5. **Product image assets = brand reference only.** If the client uploads product/lifestyle images, use them as mood/brand reference for the director. Do not attempt to composite them literally into the video unless explicitly instructed.
6. **Music at ≤10% volume.** Voice is primary. If the mix pushes music above 10%, the spokesperson's warmth is undercut.
7. **Hook overlay goes in the top zone.** TikTok UI occupies the right side and lower-right. Bottom-zone text overlays on the hook are obscured. Top zone only.
8. **Speech pacing 2.2–2.5 w/s.** Faster pacing reads as English-ad speed and loses the deliberate community-respect register.
9. **Background must include family signifiers.** Bookshelf with family photos, or equivalent warm domestic prop, in the background. This activates social proof and empathy before a word is spoken.
10. **100% Spanish, no Spanglish.** The ad is for a Spanish-language audience. Code-switching signals inauthenticity to this demographic.
