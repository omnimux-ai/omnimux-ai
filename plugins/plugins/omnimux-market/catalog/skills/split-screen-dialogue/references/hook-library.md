# Hook Library — Split-Screen Dialog Ad

The hook line is always the woman's first beat. It lands before any other line. It must follow the formula in SKILL.md §3:

> ≤ 10 words · imperative verb · names a scenario · ends on a single-word command or beat.

## Opener formula

```
[Scenario noun]. [Positioning detail]. [Action imperative]. [Single-word dare].
```

Four fragments, four periods. The rhythm: *"Rooftop bar. She's by the railing. Ten seconds. Go."*

## Niche variations

| Niche | Hook line |
|---|---|
| Dating confidence | "Rooftop bar. She's by the railing. Ten seconds. Go." |
| Cold-approach training | "Coffee shop. She's in line behind you. Thirty seconds. Open." |
| Language learning (travel) | "Tokyo. Train station. You need directions. Go." |
| Fitness accountability | "4am. Gym's open. Alarm just went off. Move." |
| Interview prep | "Tuesday. Hiring manager just walked in. Pitch yourself. Now." |
| Public speaking | "Stage lights. Two hundred people. Mic's hot. Speak." |
| Relationship repair | "She's pissed. You messed up. One sentence. Start." |
| Sales call practice | "Decision-maker on the line. Sixty seconds. Pitch. Go." |

## What breaks the hook

- Starting with a question ("So, picture this…") — kills the in-medias-res energy.
- Naming the product in the hook — feels like an ad, viewer scrolls.
- More than one full sentence — the four-fragment cadence is the trigger.
- Soft verbs ("try", "maybe", "see if") — the dare is the point.

## What the man does during the hook

Always: visible in the bottom half, silent, micro-reaction within 1s of the dare landing. Choose one:
- Eyebrow raise + half-smile (default — playful).
- Slow blink + slight lean back (overwhelmed).
- Wince + nervous laugh (anxious).

Pick based on the niche's emotional register. Default to playful unless the brand is therapy/wellness, in which case use overwhelmed.
