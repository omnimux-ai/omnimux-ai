# UGC Two-Character Comedy Skit Hook

A proven creative pattern for short-form vertical video (TikTok / Reels / YouTube Shorts) in which a relatable everyman character gets hit with a painful status-quo moment and a theatrical "expert foil" character materialises to roast them into taking action. The comedy lands because the foil is over-the-top, the everyman is under-armed, and the product becomes the punchline resolution rather than an interruption.

---

## 1. When to use this pattern

**Ideal fit:**
- Financial-services DTC (insurance, loans, credit, budgeting, banking)
- Any product that replaces an obviously bad status quo (cable bills, overpriced SaaS, gym memberships, etc.)
- Brands with a mascot, persona, or character-forward identity
- Audiences 18–35, mobile-first, TikTok / Reels primary
- Creative briefs that specify comedy, character, or roast tone

**Poor fit:**
- Premium/luxury positioning — the roast energy undercuts aspirational tone
- Healthcare / grief-adjacent categories — dark comedy misfires
- B2B SaaS with a long sales cycle — the CTA mechanic ("get a free quote below") is too soft for enterprise
- Brands with no characters and no desire to build one

---

## 2. Hook & Opening

**Structural move: Bill-shock / status-quo reveal**

The ad opens mid-moment — the everyman is already reacting to a piece of bad news (a renewal notice, a bill, a price screen). No intro, no brand reveal, no logo. The first frame IS the hook.

**Example hook (invented, generic):**
> Scene 1: Person sits at a kitchen table, opens a renewal notice, face drops. "$312/month" overlaid in red bounce-in text. No voiceover. Just the face and the number.

**Rule:** Swap the dollar amount and the product category; keep the physical reaction shot and on-screen number overlay. The number must be specific and shocking — never a range, never "too much." The visual of someone reacting to a real number is the scroll-stop.

**On-screen text style:** Bold, high-contrast, brief. Red or white. Bounce-in or punch animation. One value, 1–2 words max. Never full sentences in the hook frame.

---

## 3. Narrative Arc

**Beat structure: Shock → Dramatic Arrival → Roast Loop → Reluctant Concession → CTA**

| Beat | Target Duration | Voice/Visual Tone |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| **1. Shock reveal** — everyman encounters the painful status quo. No dialogue, just reaction + number overlay. | 3–5s | Silent or ambient SFX. Handheld close-up on face. |
| **2. Dramatic arrival** — the foil character appears from nowhere (shadows, doorway, jump cut). First line is a roast of the situation, not a product pitch. | 5–7s | Foil is theatrical, deadpan, or hyped. Two-shot or hard cut between characters. |
| **3. Roast loop** — 1–2 exchanges. Foil escalates the absurdity; everyman pushes back weakly. The product is hinted at but not named yet. | 5–7s | Fast back-and-forth. Short sentences. Comedic pauses matter — don't cut them. |
| **4. Product reveal / payoff** — foil reveals or demonstrates the solution. A visual cue makes it concrete (laptop glow, phone screen, prop). Foil delivers the "killer line." | 6–8s | Energy peaks here. Visual effect on the product reveal. Foil at full theatrical intensity. |
| **5. Reluctant concession** — everyman admits the foil is right, in ≤1 sentence. This is the social proof beat — the audience self-identifies with this moment. | 3–5s | Dry, underplayed, minimal movement. The comedy is in the restraint. |
| **6. CTA** — end card or on-camera spoken CTA. Free, low-commitment offer. | 3–5s | Either foil delivers it theatrically or an end card handles it. Never both at once. |

**Total target runtime:** 27–35 seconds.

**Pacing fingerprint:**
- Dialogue speed: ~2.0–2.7 words/second. Faster than a tutorial, slower than a hype reel.
- Fastest cut: between beat 2 and beat 3 (arrival → first roast line).
- Rhythm change: beat 5 (concession) should feel notably slower — let the silence sit for 0.5s before the CTA.
- Music: low in the mix (≤0.15 volume). Present as texture, not driver. Dramatic/comedic underscore works; lyrical music competes with dialogue.

---

## 4. Visual Style Spec

**Format:** UGC / phone aesthetic. 9:16 vertical. No brand bumpers at open.

**Camera grammar:**
- Everyman scenes: handheld, slightly imperfect framing, natural light or warm practical light. Lived-in background (apartment, desk, couch).
- Foil/character scenes: same handheld aesthetic but with dramatic lighting contrast — dim background, shadow play, rim light or single practical source. This makes the foil feel like they stepped out of a different genre.
- **Hard rule: maintain the UGC aesthetic even on the foil character.** No cinematic crane moves, no rack focus, no studio lighting. The joke is that this theatrical character exists inside a mundane phone video.

**Color palette:**
- Everyman: warm, neutral, slightly desaturated. Real-life tones.
- Foil: cooler, darker surroundings. The character itself can be high-contrast (bold uniform or costume) to pop against the dim bg.
- On-screen text: red or white for shock numbers; gold or white for CTA end card.

**Shot length distribution:**
- Hook (beat 1): single shot, 3–5s, no cut
- Dialogue beats: 1–2 cuts per beat, each shot 2–4s
- Concession: single long-ish shot, 3–5s, no cut
- End card: static or minimal motion, 3–5s

**On-screen text policy:**
- Hook frame: ONE number/stat overlay, bounce-in, red
- Captions: zoom-punch Anton style (tight, aggressive, word-by-word) — only on dialogue-heavy beats 2–4
- End card: product name wordmark + CTA line in gold or white. No body copy on end card.
- Do NOT bake text into the scene video renders. All overlays are applied at assembly.

---

## 5. Voice & Persona

**Everyman character:**
- Tone: confused, slightly defeated, relatable. Not stupid — just hasn't thought about this problem before.
- Pacing: normal conversational speed. Pauses when shocked.
- Sample rhythm: short sentences that repeat the shocking number, e.g. *"[number]? Every month?!"* — keep it brief and incredulous.
- Persona archetype: The Skeptic (not hostile, just hasn't been shown a better way yet)
- Visual: plain clothing (grey/white tee, jeans), no props that signal wealth or aspiration

**Foil/expert character:**
- Tone: deadpan-theatrical. Knows everything, slightly exasperated by the everyman, secretly delights in the roast.
- Pacing: deliberate, punchy. Lets punchlines land before moving on.
- Sample rhythm: a single metaphor-forward, confident "killer line" delivered in one breath, e.g. *"You've been overpaying for years and never noticed."*
- Persona archetype: The Dramatic Expert (competence + comedy, not aggressive)
- Visual: costume that signals expertise/role (a uniform, mascot, or themed outfit) but shot in the same UGC space

**Character contrast rule:** The comedy lives in the visual and tonal gap between the two characters. If both feel grounded, the skit loses energy. If the foil feels too polished/cinematic, the UGC frame breaks. Keep the foil theatrical-but-phone-native.

---

## 6. CTA Mechanic

**Type:** Free, low-commitment offer delivered at the close. Comment-bait is secondary; primary CTA is a link/action below.

**Wording pattern:** *"Get a FREE [thing] below 👇"* — the word FREE is always present. The action noun is specific (Quote, Check, Estimate, Scan). The down-arrow emoji points to the link.

**Delivery:** On-screen end card only (not spoken by a character in the final cut). The end card carries:
1. Brand name / wordmark
2. CTA line in bold: "Get a FREE [X] Below 👇"
3. Optional: one-line social proof ("Join X,000 people who switched")

**Hard rule:** Do not have the foil character deliver the CTA as an in-character line AND also show an end card CTA. Pick one. End card is preferred — it gives the viewer a moment to act without narrative pressure.

---

## 7. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

Treat each as a non-negotiable constraint when producing a new ad in this pattern.

1. **Two-character scenes require both voice IDs.** When a scene has two speaking characters, both `voice_asset_id` and `kling_voice_id` must be present in the director payload. A missing voice ID causes the scene to block silently. Always verify both IDs are resolved before dispatching multi-character scenes.

2. **Always `list_assets` after scene rendering before referencing outputs.** Image-type director outputs register under a different ID suffix than video outputs (e.g., `scene-6:image_hero` not `scene-6:image`). Never assume the asset ID — always query.

3. **502 errors on video generation: simplify, don't switch backends.** If a scene generation returns 502, the fix is to simplify the motion description and remove `reference_images` from that scene's payload, then retry once. Do not switch to a different backend. Do not retry with the identical payload.

4. **Comedic pauses are structural, not waste.** The concession beat (beat 5) must have 0.5s of silence before the CTA begins. Do not trim it. The laugh/recognition moment requires the pause.

5. **Music volume cap: 0.12–0.15.** Dialogue-heavy skits are killed by music that competes at or above 0.20. Set music volume at 0.12 for safety; 0.15 absolute maximum.

6. **No text baked into video renders.** All number overlays, captions, and end-card elements are applied in `assemble_video`. Scene renders are text-free. This allows easy re-versioning of CTA copy and number overlays without re-rendering video.

7. **End card image is a separate scene render, not a frame-grab.** Render the end card as an explicit image-type scene (themed motif, brand atmospheric, or simple dark BG) so it can be composited cleanly. Do not use a freeze-frame of the final video scene as the end card.

8. **The product/brand is always swappable.** The themed foil character is a brand artifact. The pattern works for any pain-point product with a free introductory offer. When adapting: swap the dollar amount, the product category, the foil persona's theme (e.g. accountant, mechanic, wizard, etc.), and the CTA noun. Keep the structural beats, the visual contrast logic, and the pacing fingerprint.
