# Content Strategy

Recommend what to create, for whom, why it should work, how it distributes, and what evidence will change the plan.

## Evidence intake

Use information already present in the request, attached files, workspace documents, and prior decisions. Ask only for unresolved facts that materially change prioritization:

- business, offer, ICP, buying reasons, and desired outcome;
- recurring customer questions, objections, support themes, and language;
- current winners/losers and available formats, people, time, and budget;
- relevant competitors and market gaps;
- requested horizon and deliverable shape.

Do not require a context file or legacy .claude path. Resolve files from the active workspace with capabilities that actually exist.

## Research policy

- Use supplied keyword exports, transcripts, surveys, analytics, and sales/support evidence first.
- Perform web/search research when the task needs current demand, competitor, trend, or SERP evidence and the runtime provides an appropriate capability.
- Prefer primary or first-party sources for platform facts; record source, date, and uncertainty.
- If fresh research is out of scope or unavailable, continue with the available evidence and label unknowns. Do not both forbid research and rely on invented search findings.

## Workflow

1. Define the customer, buying reason, business goal, distribution surface, and success signal.
2. Extract evidence-backed content opportunities and preserve the customer's own language.
3. Classify each idea as searchable, shareable, or both; map it to awareness, consideration, decision, or implementation.
4. Use the user's prioritization method. If none is specified, Customer Impact 40% + Content-Market Fit 30% + Search Potential 20% + Resource Feasibility 10% is a heuristic, not measured truth. Do not fabricate missing scores.
5. When a roadmap is requested, cluster the strongest ideas into a small set of pillars with clear product connections and internal-link logic.
6. Sequence a minimum viable roadmap that can ship, distribute, and generate readback before expanding.
7. Deliver the strategy in the response or save it to an authorized local/workspace location. Do not add a redundant write approval when the user already requested the file.

## Load reference on demand

Load `references/framework.md` when scoring ideas, building clusters, or formatting a strategy document.

## Quality bar

- Every priority topic names its audience, intent, evidence, distribution path, and measurable signal.
- Search volume alone is not strategy; customer relevance and product fit lead.
- Shareable requires a distribution mechanism, not merely an interesting idea.
- Separate observed facts, inferences, assumptions, and unknowns.
- Do not invent customer quotes, competitor performance, keyword volume, or conversion data.
- Prefer a smaller executable roadmap over a large unranked idea list.

## Output

Return only the requested, applicable parts:

1. evidence and explicit unknowns;
2. 3–5 content pillars with rationale;
3. a prioritized topic table;
4. a topic cluster/internal-link map;
5. a 30/60/90-day or user-specified sequence;
6. distribution and readback metrics.

Optional keyword CSV, editorial calendar, or SME list is produced only when requested.
