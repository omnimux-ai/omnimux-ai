# Home Service Lead Gen — Static Meta Ad Pattern

## 1. When to Use This Pattern

**Best fit:**
- B2B lead generation agency selling services to solo operators or small-team tradespeople (painters, plumbers, electricians, tilers, landscapers, cleaners)
- Offer structure: pay-per-result / pay-per-booked-job, no retainer, no lock-in
- Platform: Meta (Facebook/Instagram) static feed ads
- Goal: inbound lead form fills from business owners, not end consumers
- Audience: blue-collar business owners aged 28–55 who are sceptical of digital marketing and have been burned before

**Poor fit:**
- Consumer-facing brands (this pattern addresses the *business owner*, not their customer)
- Video-first or Reels-only campaigns (this is a static image pattern)
- SaaS or tech products without a tangible trade/outcome component
- Luxury or aspirational lifestyle brands (tone is too direct and pain-forward)

---

## 2. Discovery Interview (Run First)

Before generating any creative, conduct a structured discovery covering:

1. Agency/brand name and URL
2. Services offered and how billing works (e.g. per booked job, retainer)
3. Primary niche / trade vertical to launch with
4. ICP description: who is the ideal client? (solo operator? 2–5 person team?)
5. The core offer — what's the hook? (e.g. "fixed fee per booked job, zero ad spend from client")
6. Guarantee or risk-reversal language
7. Social proof: testimonials, client count, jobs delivered, stat highlights
8. Brand assets available (logo, colours, fonts)
9. Tone preference (casual/direct vs professional/corporate)
10. CTA destination (lead form, DM, booking link)
11. Competitive differentiation
12. Any past ad creative they liked or hated
13. Platform primary: Facebook feed, Instagram feed, or both?
14. Budget / volume of ads needed
15. Any hard rules (language, claims, visuals to avoid)

**After the interview:** fetch the brand website to pull copy, offer details, testimonials, and stats. Use the logo upload to extract brand colour hex values and logo geometry.

---

## 3. Hook & Opening

**Archetype: Pain-point identity hook** — the opening names a specific frustration, fear, or aspiration that only the exact ICP would recognise. Not generic ("grow your business"). Hyper-specific to the trade and the struggle.

**Structural move:** *"You are [identity]. You experience [very specific pain]. Here is proof that changes."*

**Generic examples (swap the subject, keep the move):**

- Identity hook: *"[Trade] — tired of slow seasons and no-shows?"*
- Struggle hook: *"Still chasing invoices late at night? There's a better way."*
- Aspiration hook: *"What if your calendar was full before the week even started?"*
- Relief hook: *"[N] jobs booked. One month. No ad spend from you."*
- Symbolic hook: A core tool of the trade mid-use, morning light — no copy needed, the visual IS the hook.

**Rule:** The trade/niche (painters, plumbers, etc.) is always swappable. The structure of naming identity + pain is locked. Never open with the agency name or a generic benefit.

---

## 4. Narrative Arc (Static Ad — Single Frame)

Static ads have an internal visual hierarchy that functions as a micro-narrative:

| Layer | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Visual/image** | Pattern interrupt — stops the scroll | Must be instantly recognisable to a tradesperson (their tools, van, hands, worksite) |
| **2. Headline / super-text** | Names the pain or aspiration | ≤8 words. High contrast. |
| **3. Body / sub-text** | Delivers proof or specifics | 1–2 lines. Stat, testimonial quote, or offer summary. |
| **4. CTA** | Tells them what to do | "Book a Free Strategy Call" |
| **5. Brand mark** | Builds trust / recall | Bottom right or centre. Small. Never dominant. |

**Narrative shapes across the batch:**
- **Pain → Proof → Offer** (conversion-focused)
- **Identity → Aspiration → CTA** (awareness / top of funnel)
- **Testimonial → Specifics → Offer** (social proof)
- **Symbolic visual only → Minimal copy** (native-feeling, low-resistance)

---

## 5. Visual Style Spec

### Register 1: Dark-Mode Typography
- Background: deep navy or brand dark (e.g. `#0B0F1E`)
- Text: white or light-grey, large, generous line-height
- No photography — pure type + brand geometry
- Use for: offer cards, stat callouts, testimonial quotes
- Feels like: a premium app UI

### Register 2: Lifestyle Photography with Overlay
- Real or photorealistic photo of the trade in action
- Semi-transparent dark overlay for text legibility
- Headline overlaid in white, bold
- Feels like: editorial / magazine

### Register 3: Documentary / UGC-Style Photorealistic
- Candid-feeling moment: tradesperson on phone, checking notifications, sitting at kitchen table
- NOT polished stock photography — slightly raw, real-world lighting
- Minimal or no copy overlay — the image carries the emotion
- Feels like: something a friend posted

### Register 4: Symbolic Still-Life
- Object-driven: a core tool of the trade, toolbox, invoice stack, morning coffee + phone
- Clean composition, natural light
- Carries the metaphor of pain or aspiration without showing a person
- Feels like: editorial product photography

**Resolution:** 1:1 (square), 2K. Always square for Meta feed.

**On-screen text policy:**
- Registers 1 & 2: text overlaid directly in the image
- Registers 3 & 4: minimal or no in-image text; copy lives in the primary text field
- Never use more than 2 font sizes in a single ad
- Brand colour palette must appear somewhere in every ad

## 6. Signal-Layer Prompt Framework

Each ad is engineered to signal to one specific psychographic slice. Generate prompts across four signal types in parallel:

| Signal Layer | What It Targets | Prompt Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | Who they are | Visual of the trade, the van, the uniform, the tools |
| **Struggle** | What's keeping them up at night | Invoice stress, slow season, phone not ringing, no-shows |
| **Aspiration** | What they want their life to look like | Full calendar, taking days off, new vehicle, family time |
| **Relief** | The moment the problem is solved | New job booking notification, calm morning, phone ringing |

**For a 25-prompt batch, distribute roughly:**
- 6–7 Identity prompts
- 6–7 Struggle prompts
- 6–7 Aspiration prompts
- 5–6 Relief prompts

**Prompt anatomy:**
```
[Photorealistic / Cinematic] [scene tied to signal layer], [trade-specific visual cue], [lighting], [camera angle], [emotional tone], [no text / minimal text], [1:1 square]
```

**Example (Relief layer, generic trade):**
> Photorealistic, a tradesperson sitting in their work van early morning, glancing at their phone with a slight smile, notification visible on screen showing a new job booking, warm sunrise light through windscreen, slightly candid angle, no overlay text, 1:1 square

**Rule:** Prompt the *emotion and scene*, not the product or service. The agency's offer is carried by the copy, not the image.

## 7. Voice & Persona

**Tone:** Casual, direct, peer-to-peer. Speaks to the tradesperson as an equal. Short sentences. Match the local market's everyday vocabulary if targeting a specific region (use sparingly — don't overdo informal slang).

**Persona archetype:** The Experienced Peer — someone who has seen the problem, knows the solution, and is sharing it without hype.

**Phrasing patterns:**
- Short declarative opening: *"You shouldn't have to chase work."*
- Specificity over vagueness: *"[N] jobs booked, fixed fee each"* not *"great results"*
- Social proof with first name + last initial + role + region: *"[First] [L]., [Trade], [Region]"*
- Soft urgency, not hard pressure: *"Only taking [N] new clients this month"* (if true/applicable)

**Avoid:**
- Hype language ("AMAZING results!", "SKYROCKET your revenue")
- Corporate register ("leverage our proprietary system")
- Vague promises without proof

---

## 8. CTA Mechanic

**Primary CTA:** "Book a Free Strategy Call" — a soft invitation, not a command.

**Mechanic:** Meta Lead Form (in-app). No external redirect unless requested.

**Copy pattern:**
- Primary text: Pain hook → 1-line proof → Offer statement → CTA
- Ad headline field: ≤5 words, benefit-forward (e.g. "More Jobs. No Ad Spend.")
- Ad description field: Reinforce risk-reversal ("No retainer. No lock-in.")

---

## 9. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

1. **Never show the agency logo as the dominant element.** Brand mark is a trust signal, not a hero. Keep it small, bottom placement.
2. **Always include a risk-reversal statement.** "No retainer, no lock-in, pay per result" must appear somewhere — this handles the core objection.
3. **Social proof must include specifics.** Name (first + last initial), trade, location, concrete result (jobs booked, revenue, time frame). Not "clients love us."
4. **ICP-specific visual language only.** Every image must feature something the target trade would immediately recognise as their world. Generic "business owner" imagery is not acceptable.
5. **Produce across all four visual registers every campaign.** Do not cluster on one register. Diversity is how Meta's algorithm gets signal diversity.
6. **Use all four signal layers per batch.** Identity, Struggle, Aspiration, and Relief must all be represented.
7. **QA every ad before delivery.** Run each generated image through the quality gate. Flag: text legibility, brand colour presence, ICP visual relevance, CTA clarity.
8. **Square format only for Meta feed.** Always 1:1. Never 16:9 or 4:5 unless explicitly requested for a different placement.
9. **Write primary text copy for every ad.** Do not deliver images without accompanying ad copy. Creative and copy are a unit.
10. **Some ads should lead with no brand name.** Identity-layer and symbolic ads especially benefit from logo-minimal or logo-free treatment — the brand appears at the end or not at all.

---

## 10. Recommended Batch Structure

| Wave | Count | Purpose | Visual Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wave 1: Foundation** | 3 ads | Brand introduction — Offer, Social Proof, Pain Point | Dark-mode typography |
| **Wave 2: Lifestyle** | 3 ads | Trade-in-action imagery, aspirational moments | Lifestyle + UGC |
| **Wave 3: Signal Matrix** | 8–20 ads | Signal-layer targeting matrix, maximum creative diversity | All four registers |

Wave 1 establishes brand recall. Wave 2 builds emotional connection. Wave 3 is the performance engine.
