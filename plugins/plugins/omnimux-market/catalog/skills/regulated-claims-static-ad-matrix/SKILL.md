# Regulated-Service — Static Ad Matrix Pattern

A high-volume, compliance-locked creative pattern for Meta cold-audience static image ads in any category where a mandated legal disclaimer must appear on the creative. Designed to flood a media buy with creative variety while holding the brand disclaimer and legal guardrails absolutely fixed.

---

## 1. When to use this pattern

**Ideal for:**
- Services whose category requires a fixed regulatory disclaimer on every ad
- Cold Meta (Facebook/Instagram) audiences who have never heard of the brand
- Campaigns needing 20–80 creative variants in a single sprint
- Any regulated-services ad where mandated language must appear verbatim on the image itself

**Not suited for:**
- Video or animated ads (use a UGC/motion pattern instead)
- Warm retargeting (the broad educational hooks over-explain to people who already know)
- Brands without a confirmed regulatory wording to anchor authority hooks

---

## 2. Hook & Opening

### Hook archetypes (use one per ad; rotate across batch)

| Archetype | Structural move | Example (invented, rewrite per client) |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Regulator news-peg authority** | Cite an official confirmation as the news hook | "The regulator has confirmed many customers were overcharged." |
| **Stat shock** | Lead with a striking number tied to money owed | "Customers could be owed money — and most don't know it." *(avoid currency figures in image; move stat to headline copy)* |
| **Relatable pain** | Mirror the audience's felt experience before naming the solution | "You paid every bill on time. But were you charged a hidden fee?" |
| **Curiosity gap** | Pose a question the reader can't ignore | "If you used this service in the last few years, your provider may owe you money." |
| **Empathy open** | Lead with fairness, not urgency | "You didn't set the terms. Someone else did — without telling you." |
| **Native UI disruption** | Dress the image as a familiar phone UI (notes app, search bar, messaging app, poll, lock screen) | "notes: things I found out about my account…" |
| **Editorial authority** | Dictionary definition, big pull-quote, or newspaper-layout typographic card | Dictionary card: *over·charge (v.) — to bill a customer more than the agreed or fair amount.* |
| **Illustration / diagram** | Venn, flowchart, or infographic that explains the mechanism visually | Venn: "What the provider knew / What you were told / The difference = your claim" |

**Swap rule:** Replace the example product/brand and category with the new client's product/brand. The structural move — the question, the authority peg, the UI format, the diagram — stays locked. Never paste the current client's exact headline word-for-word; rewrite for the new product.

## 3. Narrative Arc (for single static image)

Static ads don't have a time-based arc; they have a **visual hierarchy arc** read top-to-bottom / focal-point-out:

| Zone | Beat | Tone |
|------|------|------|
| **Primary focal point** (top 40 % or centrepiece) | Hook — the one thing that stops the scroll | Bold / surprising / human |
| **Supporting body** (middle 30–40 %) | Expand — one sentence of proof or qualification | Calm, factual, confident |
| **Compliance disclaimer** (bottom band, always present) | Lock — verbatim regulatory disclaimer | Small but legible; never cropped |
| **Implicit CTA** (via overall tone) | Signal — design implies "there's an action to take" without urgency | No timers, no "act now" |

For **carousel** formats the arc spans slides:
- Slide 1: Hook / question
- Slides 2–4: One revelatory fact or step per slide (escalating understanding)
- Slide 5: Soft resolution + implicit next step (no hard countdown CTA)

## 4. Visual Style Spec

### Format
- **1:1 (1080 × 1080 px)** — default for feed; use for typographic, editorial, photoreal still-life
- **4:5 (1080 × 1350 px)** — portrait-dominant feed placement; use for lifestyle, native UI, illustration

### Visual sub-styles (rotate across batch; never more than 30 % of batch in one sub-style)

| Sub-style | Description | When to use |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Photoreal still-life** | Real-looking objects relevant to the product: documents on a desk, magnifying glass over a contract, everyday items. Neutral/warm light. No studio-white backdrop. | Trust / discovery angle |
| **Typographic card** | Bold headline text, clean background (navy, deep teal, slate, warm cream). No photography. Kinetic-feeling layout via weight contrast. | Stat, TIL, editorial |
| **Native UI** | Mimics phone notes, search suggestions, app screen, lock screen notification, poll card. High realism. | Curiosity/scroll-stop |
| **Illustration / diagram** | Flat or semi-realistic: Venn, flowchart, handshake icon, friendly mascot. Friendly colour palette. | Process explanation |
| **Editorial layout** | Newspaper column, dictionary entry, pull-quote block. Serif or hybrid font pairing. | Authority / legitimacy |
| **Lifestyle scene** | Person on sofa with phone, person looking at paperwork at kitchen table. Warm, candid framing. No direct camera gaze unless the brief calls for UGC feel. | Emotional / relatability |

### Colour
- **Brand palette anchor:** Use the client's palette; keep contrast ratio accessible. A trustworthy default is a deep cool primary (navy/teal/slate) plus one warm accent.
- **Background:** Never pure white for photoreal; use warm off-white, light stone, or contextually appropriate surface.
- **Text on image:** High contrast only. White on dark or dark on light. No outlined text unless it suits the sub-style.

### Camera / rendering grammar
- **Photoreal:** Natural window light or soft side-fill. Shallow depth-of-field implied. Objects angled ~20–30° for dimensionality.
- **UI mockups:** Perfect flat-on perspective; full-bleed phone chrome or cropped to content area only. No tilted phone mockup.
- **Illustrations:** Clean vector or semi-realistic. Consistent line weight within one image.
- **Editorial/typographic:** Flat lay feel. No photography unless a subtle texture.

### On-screen text policy
- **Allowed:** Hook headline, one supporting line, verbatim compliance disclaimer.
- **Never:** Slide numbers, counters, countdown timers, currency figures (move to ad copy), third-party brand/partner/media logos or trade dress, product badges, regulator/official logos, celebrity likeness, review-platform logos, watermark, garbled or misspelled text, any text not explicitly specified.

## 5. Voice & Persona

**Persona archetype:** Calm-authoritative narrator with a sense of fairness — not a hype salesperson. The implicit POV is a knowledgeable friend who found out something important and is passing it on without drama.

**Tone keywords:** Factual, measured, slightly conspiratorial (you-didn't-know-but-now-you-do), empathetic, never urgent or panicked.

**Phrasing patterns that work:**
- "Most people don't realise…"
- "If you used [product] in [time window], you may be owed…"
- "The regulator confirmed…" (authority-peg opener)
- "No win, no fee. No upfront cost." (reassurance cadence: short, parallel, declarative)
- "You paid — but did you pay too much?" (question that implies unfairness without accusation)

**What to avoid:** Hyperbole ("MASSIVE payout!"), countdown urgency ("only 3 days left"), legalese jargon as headline copy, first-person testimonial unless explicitly a UGC sub-style.

---

## 6. CTA Mechanic

**Pattern:** **Soft implied CTA via design + explicit disclaimer.** No hard "click here now" on the image itself. The image creates pull; the ad copy and landing page carry the conversion action.

- On typographic / editorial ads: end with a one-line question or declarative that implies next action — e.g. "Find out if you qualify."
- On native UI ads: the UI itself is the CTA signal (a search bar with the relevant query already typed implies there's something to search for).
- On carousel: final slide may include a soft button-shaped text element ("Check your eligibility →") but no countdown, no currency figure.
- **Spoken/caption CTA:** Not applicable for static images — CTA lives in ad-level primary text, not on the image.

---

## 7. Compliance Disclaimer — Hard Rule

> **This rule cannot be softened, abbreviated, paraphrased, or omitted on any image.**

The client supplies their mandated disclaimer; treat it as fixed, verbatim text. A neutral illustrative example of the *shape* of such a disclaimer (do not ship — replace with the client's actual regulatory wording):

> *"You can pursue this matter directly with your provider at no cost. If you choose to instruct us, we act on a no win, no fee basis and charge a fixed percentage of any amount recovered. Full fee terms are available on request."*

When adapting for a different client:
- Replace the firm name and fee structure with the new client's mandated wording verbatim.
- Position: bottom band of the image, always visible, never cropped. Font size: legible at mobile preview scale (minimum ~18 px equivalent at 1080 px canvas).
- If the client has not yet provided their regulatory wording, **pause and request it before generating any images.** Do not draft placeholder disclaimer copy.

## 8. Batch Architecture (Creative Matrix)

This pattern's core value is **systematic variety, not one-off ideation.** Structure each batch as a matrix:

```
Ad types (rows) × Persuasion angles (columns) = batch size
```

### Proven ad types
1. **Document / contract still-life** — object-based, discovery angle
2. **Checklist card** — eligibility criteria, typographic
3. **Lifestyle scene** — emotional, relatable
4. **Brand typographic card** — authority, stat, or TIL hook
5. **Native UI disruption** — scroll-stop, curiosity gap
6. **Editorial / dictionary** — legitimacy, authority
7. **Illustration / diagram** — process explanation
8. **Photoreal POV** — hands-on, paperwork on a table, everyday object close-up
9. **Carousel: Timeline / scandal arc** — 5 slides walking through how the issue happened
10. **Carousel: Quiz / FAQ** — 5 slides of quick Q&A ("Did you use the service? → Yes / Not sure")

### Angle rotation (apply across ad types)
- Provider education ("Your provider set the terms — not you")
- Eligibility window ("Within the last few years — you may still qualify")
- Done-for-you simplicity ("We handle everything")
- Persistence / tenacity ("We've already recovered claims others said were too old")
- Trust / anti-scam ("We're regulated — here's how to verify")
- Audience-specific (segment by the client's relevant customer groups)
- Emotional fairness ("You trusted the process. The process let you down.")

### Batch sizing guidance
- **Batch 01 (cold test):** 5–8 ads, 1:1 only, one angle per ad type. Goal: establish format baseline.
- **Batch 02 (full matrix):** 25–35 ads, mixed 4:5 and 1:1, full angle × type matrix.
- **Batch 03 (expansion):** 35–50 ads including carousel formats, niche audiences, and competitor-displacement variants.

## 9. Competitor Ad Adaptation Rule

When the user uploads competitor ads for reference:

1. **Inspect each ad** — identify layout structure, colour palette, focal hierarchy, headline pattern, CTA placement.
2. **Rebuild faithfully** — preserve the layout concept and visual idea.
3. **Swap the disclaimer only** — replace the competitor's compliance disclaimer with the client's verbatim disclaimer. Do not copy competitor brand colours, logos, or trade dress.
4. **Never copy** competitor imagery, iconography, or exact headline wording. Structural concepts (a checklist layout, a split-screen) are fair game; brand assets are not.

---

## 10. Legal Hold Flags

Flag any image prompt for legal review (do not generate or deliver) if it:
- Names a specific third-party provider, manufacturer, or partner
- Shows or implies a specific settlement amount on the image
- Uses a real person's likeness or a named public figure
- Contains language that could be read as a guarantee of recovery
- Reproduces another brand's logo, trade dress, or copyrighted creative

Document flagged items with their index number and reason. Deliver the rest of the batch; return to flagged items only after legal clearance.

---

## 11. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

Treat each as a non-negotiable rule:

1. **Verbatim disclaimer on every image.** No exceptions. Not paraphrased. Not shortened. Not omitted "for clean design."
2. **No currency figures on image.** Move monetary amounts to ad-copy headline or body text. The image must not show any specific sum.
3. **No third-party/partner logos or trade dress.** Not blurred, not "inspired by" — not present at all.
4. **No product badges.** Generic imagery only; no brand-identifiable styling, markings, or emblems.
5. **No countdown timers.** The deadline urgency mechanic is banned in this category.
6. **No celebrity or public figure likeness.** Even implied similarity.
7. **No regulator or official government logo.** Reference the regulator by name in text only.
8. **No review-platform logos** (star-rating graphics, review badges, etc.).
9. **No slide numbers or counters** on carousel images.
10. **No misspelled or garbled text** — if generation produces text errors, regenerate; do not patch in post.
11. **Disclaimer font must be legible at mobile preview scale** — not sub-pixel fine print.
12. **Parallel dispatch** — when generating 10+ images, dispatch all prompts simultaneously, not sequentially. Do not wait for one image before prompting the next.
13. **Aspect ratio discipline** — confirm 1:1 vs 4:5 intent per image; do not default all to square without checking the placement brief.
