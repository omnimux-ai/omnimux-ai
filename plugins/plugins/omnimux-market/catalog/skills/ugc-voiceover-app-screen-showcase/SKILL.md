# UGC Voiceover — App Screen Showcase

A short-form ad pattern for apps: hook via problem overlay → rapid feature showcase → brand-color CTA card. Voice-only narrator, no face camera. Feels like a trusted friend sharing a discovery.

---

## 1. When to use this pattern

**Best fit:**
- Mobile apps with a clear before/after value prop (health, wellness, productivity, finance, fitness, beauty)
- Brands that have polished UI screenshots but no live footage
- Platforms: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts (9:16, ≤30s)
- Audiences who respond to peer-discovery tone ("found this app and honestly...")

**Poor fit:**
- Physical products that need tactile/lifestyle footage
- Enterprise/B2B SaaS (tone reads too casual)
- Brands without a distinct color palette (the CTA card depends on brand color anchoring)

---

## 2. Hook & Opening (0–8s)

**Structural move: Problem-question overlay on the hero product screen.**

The opening frame IS a screenshot of the app's most visually compelling screen (not the home screen — pick the screen that shows the core action: a timer, a scan result, a streak count). Over that screen, a minimal text overlay poses the problem as a rhetorical question.

**Invented generic example (for a neutral hypothetical product):**
> "Started journaling but never sure it's actually helping? The app fixes that."

**Swap rule:** Replace the product category and app name with your own. Keep the exact structure: `"Got/Started [thing user already has/does] but [nagging doubt]? [App name] fixes that."` The doubt must feel real, not manufactured.

**Text overlay format:** Two lines, minimal style, placed upper-center or center of frame. Use the brand's editorial font (not bold sans). The overlay should feel like a caption someone typed, not a marketing headline.

**Voiceover delivery:** Warm, slightly conspiratorial — as if the narrator is pulling the viewer aside. Mid-pace. Do NOT rush the hook. Let the problem land before the product name.

---

## 3. Narrative Arc

Beat-by-beat shape: **Problem → Multi-feature Revelation → Proof → CTA**

| Beat | Duration | What happens | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–8s | Problem question VO + hero screen + text overlay | Intimate, matter-of-fact |
| Feature reveal 1 | 8–11s | Show tracking/dashboard screen. VO names feature + immediate benefit | Warm, slightly excited |
| Feature reveal 2 | 11–14s | Show personalization/plan screen. VO: "built around you" language | Peer-to-peer, nodding |
| Feature reveal 3 | 14–17s | Show AI/coach/intelligence screen. VO: the "smart assistant" moment | Credibility-building |
| Feature reveal 4 | 17–20s | Show progress/results screen. VO: proof ("see the change happening") | Aspirational, grounded |
| CTA | 20–28s | Brand-color background + app icon or report screen. VO: full product pitch + download CTA | Clear, warm, direct |

**Target total runtime:** 28–32s. Do not pad. Each cut should earn its second.

The arc name: **"quiet discovery → stacked proof → confident invite"**. The viewer should feel they discovered something, not that they were sold to.

---

## 4. Visual Style Spec

**Format:** UGC-adjacent (feels organic) but with editorial polish. Not lo-fi shaky cam. Not cinematic hyperreal. The screen showcase IS the visual — treat it with respect.

**Screen presentation:** App screenshots fill the frame or occupy a natural phone-in-hand crop. Light vignette acceptable. Do not over-stylize (no glitch effects, no heavy color grading over UI).

**Color palette:** Lock to the brand's primary palette throughout. For wellness brands, expect warm-organic tones (coral/terracotta, sage, soft cream, or blush) — these should bleed into scene transitions and the CTA background. The CTA scene MUST use a solid brand color as the background (not white, not black).

**Camera grammar:** Locked-off on screens. Slow push-in (0.3–0.5× zoom over 3–4s) on the hero hook screen to add life. Cut-driven momentum in the feature reveal block — no wipes, no fancy transitions. Hard cuts only.

**Shot length distribution:**
- Hook scene: single sustained shot, 8s
- Feature block: 4 cuts × ~3s each = 12s total
- CTA: single sustained shot, 8s

**On-screen text policy:**
- Scene 1: Minimal overlay (problem hook), brand font, 2 lines max
- Scenes 2–4 (feature block): Kinetic word-by-word captions synced to VO (editorial-clean preset). No additional overlays competing with captions.
- CTA scene: Captions MUTED. Single pill/badge overlay ("Download Free on the App Store") at bottom-center. No other text.

**Font guidance:** Choose a font that matches brand personality. Wellness/lifestyle → serif or high-legibility editorial (e.g. Alice, Playfair, or equivalent). Avoid heavy grotesque sans for this style.

---

## 5. Voice & Persona

**Persona archetype:** Warm peer-narrator. Not an announcer. Not a hype-voice. The "friend who found something that actually works."

**Voice setup:** Female or gender-neutral, young adult (22–35 range), warm timbre. Voice-only — no face on camera.

**Tone:** Calm-authoritative with micro-moments of genuine enthusiasm. The narrator believes in the product; they're not performing belief.

**Pacing:** Moderate. Pause slightly after the hook problem line — let the question hang one beat before the app name. Accelerate slightly through the feature block (energetic but not rushed). Slow back down for the CTA.

**Sample phrasing patterns to reuse (swap product nouns):**
- Hook: `"Got [X] but [nagging doubt]? [App] fixes that."`
- Feature intro: `"It tracks every [session/rep/scan/entry] — [immediate benefit phrase]."`
- Personalization beat: `"[App] builds a plan around [your goals / your routine / your schedule] — not some generic template."`
- AI/coach beat: `"The [Smart/AI] Coach [tells you exactly what to do / adjusts as you go / answers your questions]."`
- Proof beat: `"[Progress snapshots / weekly reports / trend charts] so you can see [the change / the results / the difference] yourself."`
- CTA: `"[Product category] that's finally personal and trackable. Download [App Name] free on the App Store today."`

---

## 6. CTA Mechanic

**Type:** Direct download prompt — spoken + on-screen pill badge.

**Spoken CTA formula:** `"[One-sentence product value summary]. Download [App Name] free on the App Store today."`
- The spoken CTA must include "free" if the app has a free tier — this consistently reduces friction.
- End on "today" — not "now," not "below." The spoken rhythm of "today" closes naturally.

**On-screen:** A single pill/rounded-rectangle badge reading `"Download Free on the App Store"` (or Play Store equivalent). Position: bottom-center, clear of safe zones. No QR code. No URL.

**Background:** Solid brand color (not a screenshot). This signals the ad is "done" — the viewer's attention pivots from discovery mode to action mode. High contrast between badge text and background.

**No comment-bait.** This pattern does not ask viewers to comment or engage. It is a direct response ad. Do not add "comment X if you want to know more."

---

## 7. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

Treat each as a non-negotiable constraint when applying this skill:

1. **Voice-only narrator — no on-camera talent.** The app UI is the visual star. Adding a talking head competes with the screen showcase and changes the feel entirely.
2. **CTA scene: muted captions.** The CTA moment must be clean. Word-by-word captions on the CTA scene create visual noise that undermines the close. Use the pill badge only.
3. **Pill badge for the download prompt — not a caption, not a text overlay in the same style as hook text.** The pill/badge signals "actionable" to the viewer; plain text does not.
4. **Music under 0.15 volume.** The voiceover carries this ad. Music is texture only — ambient, instrumental, wellness-appropriate. If it's audible as music (not atmosphere), it's too loud.
5. **Brand color on CTA background — never white, never black.** The color transition from screen screenshots to a solid brand color is a deliberate pacing signal. Preserve it.
6. **Four feature screens in the feature block — not three, not five.** Four cuts at ~3s each fills 12s cleanly and creates a "stacked proof" feeling. Fewer feels thin; more feels rushed.
7. **Editorial font (not bold sans) for overlays.** Wellness brands read as approachable and credible with editorial fonts. Bold grotesque sans reads as "ad-y" and breaks the peer-discovery tone.
8. **Do not show the app's home/splash screen as the hook screen.** Pick the screen that shows the most compelling core action (timer running, scan in progress, streak count, etc.). The splash screen is forgettable; the action screen creates curiosity.
