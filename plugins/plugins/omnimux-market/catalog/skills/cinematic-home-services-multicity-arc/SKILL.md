# Cinematic Home-Services Multi-City Arc

A production pattern for home-services brands that want one premium cinematic video scalable across many local markets. All scenes are shared; only the CTA end card swaps city name (and optionally phone/URL). The result is a city-specific ad at a fraction of per-market production cost.

---

## 1. When to Use This Pattern

**Ideal for:**
- Home remodeling, renovation, kitchen/bath, flooring, roofing, HVAC, pool, landscape companies
- Brands serving 3–20+ zip-code-defined markets under one regional identity
- Campaigns that need a premium, authoritative tone (not viral/UGC)
- YouTube pre-roll, OTT/CTV, Facebook/Instagram mid-funnel, local TV digital companion
- Products where the physical before-to-after transformation *is* the proof

**Avoid when:**
- The brand's conversion mechanic is humor, irony, or shock (use UGC-skeptic pattern instead)
- The product requires real testimonials on camera (add a testimonial beat or use a different arc)
- Each market has genuinely different offers, pricing, or services (then each city needs its own VO)
- Target audience skews under 30 and platform is TikTok/Reels (wrong visual register)

---

## 2. Hook & Opening

**Archetype: Pain-State Establishing Shot**

The first frame shows the *problem space* the audience already lives in — the ugly kitchen, the cracked driveway, the peeling bathroom. No narration yet. Let the visual carry the recognition moment for 2–3 seconds before VO begins.

**Structural move:** Slow push-in or subtle dolly toward the pain. Do NOT cut fast here. The audience needs to feel seen before they feel sold.

**Generic example brief:**
> Scene 1 brief: "Dated, worn kitchen — outdated oak cabinets, laminate countertops, harsh overhead fluorescent lighting. Slow push-in toward the peninsula. No people. Warm but tired light."

**Swap rule:** Replace "kitchen" / "outdated oak cabinets" with whatever the new client's pain state is (cracked driveway, popcorn ceiling, moldy bathroom tile). Keep the camera move (slow push-in) and the no-people rule.

**VO tone at open:** Calm, low, unhurried. No hard sell. First sentence acknowledges the problem, not the product.
> Example pattern: "If your [space] still looks like [decade], you're not alone."

## 3. Narrative Arc

**Beat structure: Problem → Proof-of-Work → Reveal → Social Proof → Hero Money Shot → CTA**

| Beat | Target Duration | Visual | VO Tone |
|------|----------------|--------|---------|
| 1. Pain-state open | 8–12s | Slow push on "before" space | Calm, empathetic, low |
| 2. Transformation in progress | 7–9s | Work-in-progress chaos — materials, tools, mid-install | "This is where we come in" energy |
| 3. First product reveal | 8–10s | Key material close-up → lateral pan to broader view | Confident, feature-naming |
| 4. Second product reveal | 8–10s | Macro detail → pull-back reveal of combined elements | Building momentum |
| 5. Accent/detail beat | 8–10s | Specialty feature (lighting, tile, hardware) | "Notice the difference" |
| 6. Full-room reveal | 10–14s | Wide cinematic arc or orbital move showing finished space | Peak energy, declarative |
| 7. Lifestyle moment | 8–12s | One real-looking human in the finished space, natural behavior | Warmth, aspiration |
| 8. Hero money shot | 8–10s | Slow push from threshold — definitive establishing view | Quiet confidence, tag line |
| 9. CTA end card | 6–10s | Branded card — city name, checklist, contact | Spoken CTA + on-screen text |

**Total runtime target:** 75–95 seconds. Under 75s feels rushed; over 100s loses pre-roll audiences.

**Narrative arc name:** *Quiet-build-to-reveal crescendo.* The pacing accelerates through beats 3–6, then deliberately slows for beats 7–8 before the hard landing on beat 9.

---

## 4. Visual Style Spec

**Register:** Cinematic real-estate / architectural videography. NOT UGC. NOT handheld shaky. NOT talking head.

**Camera grammar:**
- Gimbal-stabilized or slider/dolly only. No handheld.
- Moves: slow push-in, lateral pan, dolly pull-back reveal, orbital arc, threshold push. One move per scene — don't combine.
- Locked-off beauty shots are allowed for macro/detail beats.
- No drone (unless exterior establishing is needed — not in base pattern).

**Shot length distribution:**
- Opening beats (1–2): 8–12s each, slower pacing
- Reveal beats (3–5): 8–10s each, steady momentum
- Climax beats (6): 10–14s, let the reveal breathe
- Lifestyle + hero (7–8): 8–10s each
- NO cuts under 6 seconds in this pattern — cutting fast undermines the premium register

**Color and light:**
- Warm golden-hour natural light or warm artificial practical lighting
- Palette: warm whites, natural wood tones, muted greens/grays, no oversaturated or HDR-punchy grades
- Finished spaces should feel inviting and aspirational, not magazine-cold

**People rule:**
- Scenes 1–6, 8: NO people. Architecture and product are the subject.
- Scene 7 only: one lifestyle human, natural behavior (drinking coffee, cooking), do NOT look at camera
- CTA card: no people

**On-screen text:**
- Caption style: editorial-clean preset, word-by-word reveal, Open Sans or similar humanist sans
- Captions appear only during VO — do NOT run captions over music-only b-roll
- CTA card text: brand name in Montserrat (or equivalent) in white/cream; city line in warm gold (#C9A84C or similar); service checklist in Open Sans; contact pill with subtle gold tint
- No kinetic typography or fast-cut text animations — wrong register

**Music:**
- Modern uplifting cinematic instrumental, no lyrics
- Volume: -20dB relative to VO (approximately 0.12 normalized mix)
- Music starts with scene 1; fade up from silence is acceptable for first 2s
- Music should land a resolution or swell as the hero money shot hits

---

## 5. Voice & Persona

**Persona archetype:** Calm authoritative narrator — no persona on-camera, VO only.

**Gender / age / tone:** Professional male or female, mid-career voice, confident low delivery, unhurried cadence. NOT announcer-y. NOT hype. Think "trusted local expert" not "radio spot."

**Pacing:** Deliberate. Allow half-beat pauses between sentences. Do not rush through features. Silence between beats is a tool.

**Phrase rhythms that work in this pattern:**
- Short declarative sentences for reveals: "White shaker cabinets. Quartz countertops. Subway tile backsplash."
- Rhetorical acknowledgment at open: "If your kitchen still looks like decades ago, you're not alone."
- Quiet confidence at close: "This is what your home deserves."
- CTA spoken simply, no urgency pressure: "Visit [website] to start your project."

**What to avoid:**
- "But wait, there's more" energy
- Superlatives without visual proof ("the best remodeler in the region")
- Filler transitions ("And so…", "Now…", "Next up…")

---

## 6. CTA Mechanic

**Type:** On-screen card + spoken soft close. No comment-bait. No urgency scarcity language.

**Card anatomy (required elements):**
1. Brand name — large, centered, light color on dark background
2. **City name line** — this is the ONLY swappable element across market versions; warm gold color
3. Services checklist — 3–5 bullet points, Open Sans, smaller weight
4. Contact pill — website URL + phone number, subtle brand-color tint

**Background:** Deep solid or gradient in brand's dark anchor color (deep navy, charcoal, forest green). Avoid white backgrounds on CTA cards — they lose authority.

**Spoken CTA:** Simple, one sentence, soft. Do NOT say "call now" or "limited time."
> Pattern: "Visit [domain] to schedule your free consultation in [city]."

**City swap mechanics:** When producing N city versions:
- All video scenes (beats 1–8) are identical across cities — render once, reuse
- Re-assemble once per city, injecting only the CTA card with city name swapped
- Phone number and URL remain constant across cities unless client specifies otherwise

---

## 7. Hard Rules / Do-Not-Regress

These rules must be preserved when using this skill:

1. **No people except one lifestyle beat.** Scenes outside the designated lifestyle beat must be architecture/product only. Adding people to reveal scenes cheapens the cinematic register.

2. **No handheld or shaky cam.** Every shot must feel stabilized. Gimbal, slider, or locked-off only.

3. **Music volume stays under the VO.** Mix at approximately 0.10–0.15 normalized; the VO must always be cleanly intelligible.

4. **City name is gold, not white.** The city-differentiator line on the CTA card uses warm gold (#C9A84C or equivalent) — it must visually pop as the localization signal.

5. **CTA card background must be dark.** Deep navy, charcoal, or brand-dark. No light or white CTA backgrounds.

6. **Caption style: editorial-clean, word-by-word only.** Do not use line-by-line captioning or kinetic text in this pattern.

7. **Render scenes once, assemble per city.** Do not re-generate scenes for each city. The asset reuse is a core efficiency of this pattern.

8. **Output format: 16:9 landscape (1920×1080).** This pattern targets YouTube, OTT, and Facebook in-stream. Do not use 9:16 vertical for this arc.

9. **Total duration: 75–95 seconds.** Do not pad with extra b-roll or cut beats to hit under 75s.

10. **No urgency/scarcity language in CTA.** Soft spoken close only. The brand positioning is premium and trustworthy, not high-pressure.
